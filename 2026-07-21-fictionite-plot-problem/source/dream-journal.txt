I arrived alone in a city. Recently, many of my dreams have begun with me flying around unfamiliar cities using superpowers, but this time I decided to enter a museum.

I had been thinking about how many fictional universes contain some kind of imaginary material or energy source that allows impossible things to exist alongside an otherwise recognizable world. With that idea in mind, I imagined a substance called Fictionite—a rare crystal or stone that made fictional impossibilities possible.

Inside the museum, I found a crystal of Fictionite displayed inside a glass case. While I was examining it, a group of highly advanced robotic bikers suddenly crashed through one of the museum walls. They wore leather jackets and looked like members of a futuristic motorcycle gang. One of the robotic bikers smashed the display case and stole the Fictionite crystal.

I did not attempt to stop him.

The biker looked at me in confusion and asked, “Aren’t you going to stop us, hero?”

I told him no. The situation already seemed impossible enough without the crystal. I did not know what he expected to accomplish with the Fictionite, because a robot as advanced as him probably already depended on Fictionite in some way. The entire world might have been partially composed of it.

The robot became confused, so I explained that robots in the waking world were nowhere near as advanced as he was. A machine capable of thinking, speaking and behaving exactly like him was already something that would normally be impossible. Even if advanced robots were not completely beyond possibility, he was clearly far beyond anything currently existing in the waking world. That meant he was probably already Fictionite-powered.

I asked him what he believed the crystal would actually allow him to do. What would it change?

He reasoned that if Fictionite allowed impossible things to exist in fictional worlds, possessing a crystal of it should allow him to perform even more impossible feats.

I told him that I did not necessarily see how. We were already standing in a world filled with superpowers, advanced robots and countless other impossibilities. I could not imagine what he considered to be even more impossible than the things that already surrounded us.

The robot began reconsidering whether stealing the Fictionite was worthwhile.

Before he could make a final decision, a woman entered the room. She had extremely tall, feathery purple hair and wore a dress made from pearlescent blue fish scales. She released an evil cackle, snatched the Fictionite from the robotic biker and declared that she would show us its true power.

I told the robot to watch.

Then I looked at the woman and said, “Okay. Do something with it.”

She stared at me and asked, “What?”

I repeated that she should demonstrate the crystal’s power. I challenged her to do anything with it that she could not have done before.

She immediately began to look nervous.

I explained that we already lived in a world where I could essentially do anything. I did not believe the Fictionite would suddenly give her useful new powers, and I certainly did not believe it would allow her to defeat me. Even if she somehow managed to do something dangerous with it, I could simply act like a superhero, stop her and defeat her immediately.

Despite her nervousness, she remained somewhat cocky and still seemed convinced that the Fictionite would eventually do something extraordinary for her.

To demonstrate my point, I summoned a large collection of balloons and an enormous chocolate ice cream cake covered in fluffy white frosting. I created a second cake just for myself, and then I summoned an entire crowd of people into the museum so that the room transformed into a full birthday party.

The purple-haired woman panicked. She dropped the Fictionite crystal and ran away.

I told the robotic biker that I simply did not think stealing the crystal made sense as a plot. Neither of the villains had any clear idea of what they could actually accomplish with it, especially in a world where impossible things already happened constantly.

The robotic biker and I remained at the birthday party together. We hung out, and I ate some of the chocolate ice cream cake.

It tasted pretty good.
