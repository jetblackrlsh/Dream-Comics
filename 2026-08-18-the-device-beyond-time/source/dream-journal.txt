Dream Journal Reconstruction

My dream began in a magical study. Unlike many of my recent dreams, I was not flying through a city like a superhero. Instead, I was contemplating the mechanics of the Storyverse, much as I had done in the previous night’s dream while investigating magical crystal swords. This time, however, my attention was focused on the future technology incorporated into those swords.

I already understood that the magical crystals came from different worlds throughout the Storyverse, but I kept wondering why the technology paired with them was always described specifically as “future tech.” The wording implied more than technology from an unusually advanced civilization. It seemed to suggest a genuine connection to time travel. I began examining the object I had always regarded as the hilt of a Rainbow Crystal Sword and wondered whether it was actually a sword hilt at all. On its own, it resembled a handheld, T-shaped device with a red scanner that could almost have been mistaken for a button. I understood that this technology was called Adapt Tech.

I spent a long time analyzing Adapt Tech and considering what would make it extraordinarily innovative, even by the standards of the waking world. It was a handheld device capable of scanning other technology, identifying information about it, and copying its functionality. This made it an everything device that learned from the technology in its immediate environment. If I needed the functions of a laptop, I would not need to buy one; I could scan a nearby laptop, and the Adapt Tech could perform the same kinds of tasks. If I needed a Wi-Fi router, I could scan one, and the device could function as a router.

Adapt Tech could also integrate itself into existing objects and adapt to them. This assimilation ability allowed it to combine seamlessly with magical stones, even though magic and technology were fundamentally different systems. That distinction made me question why the Rainbow Crystal Sword of Life—my usual weapon and the most powerful artifact in the Storyverse—needed a physical crystal blade at all. If Adapt Tech could scan another device and reproduce its functions, perhaps it could simply scan the Rainbow Crystal blade and use its magic without being attached to it.

Using my Lucid Light, I imagined a separate Adapt Tech device into existence. It looked identical to the handle of the Rainbow Crystal Sword of Life, but it was not connected to any magical crystal. I held it near the sword and attempted to scan the magical blade.

The experiment failed almost immediately. Instead of acquiring the magical properties of the Rainbow Crystal, the separate Adapt Tech device began transforming into a strange, blocky substance made of blue, brown, and green crystal-like material. The same material spread across my entire body. Both the device and I seemed to glitch uncontrollably, leaving me transformed into a bizarre rocky figure.

I managed to shut the Adapt Tech down, causing the crystalline glitch material to disappear and allowing me to return to normal. From the failure, I discovered an important limitation: Adapt Tech could not scan magical things and copy their magic. Its assimilation function worked exceptionally well with magic, which was why the Rainbow Crystal Sword functioned so effectively, but its scanning function could not reproduce magical properties. The magical crystal blade was therefore not redundant. Crystal swords were special precisely because Adapt Tech and magic had to be joined together; the technology could cooperate with magic, but it could not reduce magic to something that could simply be scanned and copied.

After returning to normal, I unexpectedly began what felt like spirit-hopping. I suddenly found myself inhabiting the bodies of several different women in different time periods. I did not look like myself because, in those moments, I was literally someone else. Each transition felt like possessing another person’s body so completely that I could partially forget my original identity and fully identify as the woman I had become.

Every woman I inhabited was involved with Adapt Tech. They were investigating it, attempting to understand why technology from the future had been sent backward through time, or trying to use it to help people. Some of them were being hunted because they possessed it. I could not remember every specific event clearly because these portions of the dream were less lucid. I still made choices while occupying their bodies, but I was no longer manipulating the dream through Lucid Light.

A connecting thread repeatedly allowed me to regain lucidity and return to my own body in the magical study. I would involuntarily become a woman in another place and time, find myself caught in a situation involving the investigation or use of Adapt Tech, and then follow that conceptual connection back into my own body, where I would continue studying the device. Before long, another involuntary hop would pull me into someone else again.

I began to suspect that Adapt Tech connected people across time throughout the Storyverse. Perhaps anyone who interacted with it—or even concentrated on it—became linked to every other Adapt Tech user. This connection could explain why future technology was scattered throughout many different eras. Adapt Tech might not be rooted in any single period. Its ability to adapt could extend beyond ordinary dimensional boundaries, giving it a loose existence within the timeline and allowing both the technology and the people connected to it to travel or reach across time.

This reminded me of what I had learned about Portal Crystal blades during the previous night’s dream. I had been hunted by a ninja assassin carrying a Portal Crystal blade while I contemplated how those weapons worked. Portal Crystals were fundamentally associated with connection. They could connect different places, but perhaps they could also connect thoughts. Merely thinking about Portal Crystals could establish a connection that allowed another person possessing one to sense me and open a portal to my location. That could be how the ninja assassins who protected the secrets of the Portal blades discovered anyone investigating them.

Adapt Tech seemed to operate according to a similar principle, but across time and identity. Thinking about or using it connected me to its other users, allowing me to hop through time and embody different people. The women and I were all trying to understand why Adapt Tech had been sent into the past, but perhaps that framing was mistaken. It might never have been deliberately sent backward at all. Part of its adaptive nature could simply exist beyond the conventional limits of time, allowing it to appear throughout history and connect everyone who encountered it.

By the end of the dream, I understood that Adapt Tech was extraordinarily powerful but also deeply mysterious. It could scan and reproduce ordinary technology, assimilate successfully with magic, and potentially link technology, thoughts, users, bodies, and identities across time. It also had firm limitations, as its disastrous attempt to scan a magical crystal had demonstrated. I had learned much more about what Adapt Tech could do, but its true origin and the full reason for its presence throughout the timeline remained unresolved.
