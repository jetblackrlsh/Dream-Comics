# Page Script — The Unicode Beneath the World

All story text appears inside unnumbered rectangular caption boxes generated directly in the art. No speech bubbles, thought bubbles, floating dialogue, subtitles, watermarks, logos, numbered captions, or issue numbers. Every image is a complete 4:5 portrait high-saturation anime comic page. Captions must remain readable, clearly separated, long enough to make every story change obvious, and nonredundant. Jet and every recurring design must follow the character bible exactly. The Unicode source characters are intentional visual world-data rather than dialogue or decorative prose.

## Page 1 — Cover

One dramatic vertical composition. Jet stands at a dark terminal, seen three-quarters from behind but with his face visible in profile, reaching toward luminous rows of Unicode with one hand. Behind him, the royal academy peels apart into cyan, emerald, violet, and white glyph streams. The king and his wife fade into wireframe code at upper left; rowdy students and the guarded rear door dissolve at upper right. The copper-coil flying machine floats above Jet's open black cross-body bag, its rainbow Morphium core echoing a rainbow data trace on the terminal. Visible cover text only: “THE UNICODE BENEATH THE WORLD” and “JULY 26, 2026”. No issue number.

## Page 2 — A Role Already Written

Five panels. Jet lies conscious but immobilized during sleep paralysis in a dark bedroom as a faint violet dream threshold opens above him. He crosses into a grand royal academy, but the lucid glow immediately fades from his eyes. The older king stands in the headmaster-throne office and is clearly treated as both Jet's father and ruler. The elegant queen-consort gives Jet a small stack of requested academy records. Jet accepts them despite visible reluctance, then walks obediently into the school corridor.

Caption text:

- “I induced lucidity through sleep paralysis, but I lost it almost as soon as the dream began.”
- “I found myself attending a strange academy governed by a king who was also my father.”
- “Inside that reality, obedience felt built into my role. I served him because he was both my parent and my ruler.”
- “His aloof wife carried herself like a queen, and the two of them regularly gave me tasks.”
- “Even when I did not want to help, I usually did what they asked out of respect for my father's authority.”

## Page 3 — The Almost-Flying Machine

Six panels. The dapper blue-eyed instructor presents the flying-machine assignment in a chaotic workshop classroom. Students shout and misbehave around Jet, but he focuses at his bench. Jet shapes living rainbow Morphium around an oval copper frame. He locks two spring-loaded magnetic coils into position as blue-white arcs connect them. A peripheral smoky vial suggests Amnesium but remains half-hidden behind loose tools. Final wide panel: the completed-looking machine rises several inches above the table, wobbles hopefully, then begins to settle.

Caption text:

- “Our class assignment was to build a device that could fly by using magnetic coils.”
- “The instructors blended familiar faces, including a cool, dapper art teacher with blue eyes and a curled mustache.”
- “The classroom was rowdy, but I refused to let anyone pull my attention away from the project.”
- “I shaped rainbow-colored Morphium around spring-loaded coils, using an old dream substance to support a new machine.”
- “A trace of memory-erasing Amnesium may also have been nearby, although forgetting it was part of its danger.”
- “The machine nearly lifted itself into stable flight. I could feel that the underlying idea was sound.”

## Page 4 — Unfinished, Then Unseen

Six panels. The flying machine gently settles without crashing. Jet folds its fins and carefully places it intact inside his cross-body bag. He carries the requested records through the headmaster-throne office and hands them to the king and queen-consort. They turn away without praise, recline on separate royal couches, and fall asleep. Jet remains standing with an empty hand, visibly disappointed. Final panel: the academy clock signals day's end while Jet looks toward a shadowed rear corridor instead of the official front gate.

Caption text:

- “The device never fully worked, but I did not dismantle it or treat the attempt as a permanent failure.”
- “I folded the unfinished machine into my bag so I could return to it later.”
- “Then I completed another request for my father and his wife and gave them exactly what they wanted.”
- “Once they received it, they showed no interest in me or in the effort I had made.”
- “They simply fell asleep, leaving me without acknowledgment, feedback, or any meaningful reaction.”
- “Their indifference disappointed me, and when school ended I began searching for another way out.”

## Page 5 — The Back Way Out

Six panels. Jet moves through a shadowed rear corridor and cracks open a heavy service door. He waits while two academy security officers pass, then slips behind them into golden-hour light. Outside, rowdy classmates socialize in a pickup lane while vehicles approach. Jet listens from a distance but chooses not to join them. He walks away alone—not sad, but certain these are not his people. In the final panel, the edge of a student's body and part of the academy wall reveal faint Unicode glyphs beneath their surfaces.

Caption text:

- “Leaving the academy did not feel permitted, so I slipped through a rear exit and sneaked past security.”
- “Outside, the rowdy students from class gathered in a pickup lane and talked while waiting for rides.”
- “I heard fragments of their conversations, but I had no desire to join them.”
- “I was not rejecting friendship itself. I simply did not think I liked these particular people.”
- “As I chose distance and moved away, my awareness began to change.”
- “The world still looked solid, but something artificial was starting to show through it.”

## Page 6 — The World Becomes Characters

Six escalating panels. Thin cyan and white symbols crawl over the pickup lane. Students become half-real figures filled with flowing Unicode rows. The academy arches and vehicles flatten into wireframes defined by glyphs. Jet turns in astonishment as the entire environment strips away layer by layer. The final large panel shows Jet seated before the dark source terminal in a black void while the last transparent fragment of the academy disappears behind him.

Caption text:

- “I did not clearly announce that I was dreaming, and I did not summon my usual Lucid Light.”
- “Instead, I became certain that the objects and people around me were not made from ordinary matter.”
- “Characters and symbols began bleeding through every visible surface.”
- “The students, the pickup area, and the academy dissolved into streams of encoded information.”
- “Then the entire scene vanished.”
- “I was seated before a terminal, staring at a screen filled completely with Unicode.”

## Page 7 — The Source of Everything

Five panels around the terminal. Jet studies the luminous screen, his face reflected between authentic-looking glyph rows. A section of code projects a wireframe academy. Another projects the king and students. A rainbow-marked sequence projects Morphium and the copper-coil flying machine. Multiple code regions shift while their projected objects move and transform, proving it is a living system. Jet traces correspondences with his finger but cannot isolate which character controls which property.

Caption text:

- “Once I recognized the Unicode, I knew with absolute certainty that it represented the world I had just inhabited.”
- “The academy, the king, the students, the magnetic coils, and perhaps even Morphium had emerged from this information.”
- “The text was not static. It described motion, transformation, behavior, and continuous change.”
- “I had reached the hidden controls beneath the dream, but I still could not decipher how each character mapped to reality.”
- “Awareness had given me access to the source. Understanding it would be the next step.”

## Page 8 — One Character from Rewriting Reality

Six panels arranged around a large vertical near-edit. Jet sets his intact flying machine beside the terminal, visually connecting the two unfinished projects. He raises one hand toward a selected cluster of Unicode. A single cyan glyph brightens beneath his fingertip, and the ghostly wireframe academy subtly bends in response. Jet's expression changes from analysis to thrilled certainty. Before he can move the character, a red alarm waveform cuts across the terminal. The code fractures into warm morning light; final panel shows Jet awake in bed, reaching toward the place where the interface vanished.

Caption text:

- “A deeper form of lucidity seemed possible: I might rewrite the dream directly instead of reshaping it with Lucid Light.”
- “Like my almost-flying machine, the interface was unfinished knowledge rather than a disproven idea.”
- “I chose one character and reached forward to test my first change.”
- “For one instant, the world-outline responded beneath my hand.”
- “Before I could move the symbol or learn what it controlled, my alarm went off.”
- “I awoke knowing I had reached the foundation of the dream—only one moment too early to rewrite it.”
