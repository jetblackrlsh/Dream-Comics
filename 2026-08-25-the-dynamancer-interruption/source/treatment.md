# Treatment - The Dynamancer Interruption

## Logline

A lucid creator designing a world of frog-like concept mages loses control of his dream and must defend an honest moral boundary when a former bully's plea for redemption becomes a literal demonic attack.

## Premise

Jet enters one of his lucid “God dreams,” where he creates and studies a new Storyverse world rather than adventuring through an existing one. Inspired by his waking question about what overnight event could make reality feel like a living comic book, he imagines data centers that cool themselves with swamp water containing traces of frog DNA.

Pollution transforms ordinary people into humanoid frogs with special conceptual powers. Jet calls them Dynamancers: each person can manipulate one governing idea, such as love or knowledge, while the possible applications of that idea remain almost limitless.

## World conflict

The transformation makes the debate surrounding data centers literal. Supporters see a new era in which imagination and concepts become real power, echoing Jet's belief that AI expands human capability. Opponents warn that every new superhero also creates the possibility of a supervillain. Even undeniable superpowers cannot unite a divided world.

## Loss of control

Jet abruptly loses lucidity and finds himself living at a modern academy that is inexplicably filled with monitors, electronics, and toys from the 1990s. A power problem affects the monitor equipment inside his dorm.

Armored paramilitary guards escort a former school bully into the room. The man fixes the equipment, then presents Jet with a release form. Within the dream, he has accidentally killed someone and must perform good deeds until somebody signs for his freedom.

## Moral refusal

Jet cries because he cannot sign. He recognizes that the man may be trying to change and tells him that somebody else may eventually grant him another chance. However, their painful history means that one helpful repair cannot make Jet personally responsible for declaring him redeemed. Jet maintains the boundary without claiming that the man is permanently beyond redemption.

Jet begins calling him by the name of a fictional television character he likes. The character fought demons and faced a similar trial in which continued good deeds determined whether he could live freely.

## Transformation and attack

The conceptual comparison becomes physically real. Red-and-blue slimy tendrils cover the former bully like a symbiote, transforming him into a demon. The paramilitary guards are suddenly gone, and the creature attacks Jet with objects from the dormitory.

Jet has lost his cosmic authorial control, but the retro environment gives him a practical weapon. He finds a bizarre full-sized motorized zip-line toy and sets it up during the chase.

## Climax

Jet rides the motorized zip line into the academy hallway. Using its momentum, he kicks the demonic symbiote through a high window and defeats him.

## Ending

The instant the creature falls, Jet regains lucidity and returns to his creator's vantage point above the frog-Dynamancer world. Nothing there has changed. He marvels at the entire academy story that overtook him while his control was absent, resumes observing the world, senses that morning is close, and chooses to wake.

## Theme

Concepts can become power, but power does not erase personal boundaries or guarantee moral transformation. Jet's imagination creates superheroes at a cosmic scale, while his integrity protects him when he is forced inside the story.

## Emotional arc

Jet moves from playful cosmic authorship and intellectual curiosity into grief, discomfort, and danger. After refusing coerced forgiveness, he survives through improvisation rather than godlike power. He returns to lucidity with both his creative agency and his boundary intact.
