Dream Journal Reconstruction

I found myself inside an enormous library. The space seemed almost infinite, with shelves and books extending far beyond anything I could fully take in. Large glass windows opened the library to a view of the night sky outside, making the entire place feel vast, quiet, and otherworldly.

At first, I was confused about who the head librarian actually was. A knight in a suit of armor was walking around the library and shelving books, so it appeared to be the one doing the librarian’s work. At the same time, there was a woman seated at what looked like the front desk. She looked almost like a witch, although not an ugly or frightening one. She had greenish-blue eyes and long, pale-blue hair that sparkled. She wore sky-blue robes decorated with small, colorful gemstones. Even though she was sitting at the front desk, she seemed to be relaxing rather than working. She was absorbed in a book she was reading.

I approached her and asked what she was reading.

She told me that she was reading her life book. She explained that this was a library of life books, containing books that described everyone’s life in detail.

I was immediately fascinated. I asked her how the book was.

She said that she read it every day. According to her, it was a little boring, but it was also confusing and filled her with uncertainty. I asked what about it made her feel that way.

She explained that much of a person’s life book contained things they already remembered about their own life. Reading those portions felt redundant, which made them boring. Other portions felt almost wrong because the events were not written the way she remembered experiencing them. Then there were the portions about the future. Those parts never remained fixed. They were constantly being rewritten and functioned more like estimates of what might happen later. Only after something actually happened did that part of the book become settled.

I found the entire idea deeply fascinating.

I then asked her about the knight. It seemed strange that the knight was walking through the library, carrying and shelving the books, while she remained seated at the desk reading. She explained that she experienced a special form of sleep that was different from the way everyone else rested. During the daytime, she rested her magic while continuing to work physically. She personally moved around the library and shelved the books. During the nighttime, she rested her physical body while continuing to use her magic. She animated the suit of knight’s armor so that it could perform the library’s physical work while her body rested.

She told me that caring for the enormous library was effectively her responsibility. Animating the knight’s armor was a tremendous help, but maintaining the animation still required her magic. Because of that, she was never able to rest completely. During one part of the day she rested her magic but not her body, and during the other part she rested her body but not her magic. Either way, she could only half rest.

I wanted to help her. I drew Silonis, the Rainbow Crystal Sword of Life. Because Silonis could bring inanimate objects to life, I believed I could use it to make the suit of armor into a genuinely living being. If the armor became alive, the librarian would no longer need to spend her magic animating it throughout the night.

I used Silonis on the suit of armor.

The moment it came to life, the knight went ballistic. It dropped the books it had been carrying and raced directly toward the librarian. I immediately used Silonis to create a rainbow crystal force field around it. The force field formed a sealed shield bubble, trapping the knight inside before it could reach her.

I demanded to know what this meant.

Instead of answering normally, the librarian became completely fixated on Silonis. She grabbed the sword tightly and exclaimed, “Oh, baby, you’ve returned.” She referred to the sword by its name and spoke as though she knew it personally. Her behavior suggested that she and Silonis shared memories from some previous time.

From inside the rainbow crystal bubble, the knight revealed the truth. The librarian had animated it to serve her, effectively making it her slave. For a while, the knight had accepted its existence in the library. Eventually, however, it had asked to be released and allowed to leave. Rather than granting its freedom, the librarian had placed a spell on it that suppressed its free will. It had been waiting for a very long time for an opportunity to become free, and now that Silonis had transformed it into a genuinely living being, all of its buried anger had erupted.

I looked back at the librarian and realized that she was not entirely in her right mind. Something had corrupted her. She continued clutching Silonis desperately, refusing to let go while speaking about memories she claimed to share with the sword.

Silonis rejected her.

The sword pulled itself out of her grasp and flew back to me. The librarian stared after it in disbelief. “After everything we’ve been through, I can’t believe that you would do this,” she said. She then tried to chase after it.

Once Silonis returned to my hand, I turned the sword toward her and stabbed her with it. Silonis did not have to harm someone when it pierced them. It was the Sword of Life, capable of healing and purification, so the strike did not truly injure her. Instead, I used the blade to cleanse the darkness from within her while healing her at the same time.

For a moment, it appeared to work. The corruption seemed to leave her, and I thought she had been purified.

Almost immediately, however, the darkness crawled back over her. Her eyes turned red, and the corrupting presence reasserted itself. Speaking through the librarian, it told me that it kept jumping from person to person in an attempt to gain possession of the Rainbow Crystal Sword and reunite with it. No matter what it tried, it could not find a way to take Silonis from me.

As I faced it, I understood that this was the same darkness I had been encountering within different beings across my dreams. Silonis was the most powerful artifact in the Storyverse because it was the Sword of Life and could bring things to life. I had always sensed that some corrupting force surrounded the desire for the sword. Previously, I had thought people might be driven by ordinary greed, by the sword’s reputation, or by the immense power it represented. Now I began to realize that it was not merely greed or fame driving them. The same darkness had been moving through different hosts, repeatedly pursuing Silonis and trying to separate it from me.

Rather than remain with the corrupted librarian, I took off into the air. I flew through one of the library’s immense glass windows and out into the Storyverse, leaving the librarian and the darkness behind.

That was the end of the dream.
