Dream Journal Reconstruction

Last night’s dream began with me thinking extensively about dungeons. The day before, I had played a game called Scoundrel for the first time. Scoundrel uses solitaire cards to simulate a dungeon crawl. I had also bought thirty-two classic Dungeons & Dragons games. Some of them were designed to provide fairly freeform Dungeons & Dragons experiences in which I could go on procedurally generated dungeon-crawl adventures, simulate controlling my own D&D kingdom, and even create my own dungeons. With all of that fresh in my mind, it made sense that one of the first things I thought about after falling asleep was a dungeon.

I had often found fantasy dungeons conceptually confusing. They usually involved entering a place filled with monsters in search of treasure, but I did not understand why those places were called dungeons. A dungeon was a prison. A place intended to hold treasure and money would normally be a vault, and a vault could reasonably have security systems protecting it. It did not make much sense to store treasure in a prison. In the dream, I began trying to create a dungeon whose combination of monsters and treasure would have a logical explanation.

As I thought through the idea, a Storyverse world began building itself around me through my Lucid Light. I decided that the dungeon had originally been a monster zoo. Long ago, people had been able to visit it and see many different kinds of powerful monsters. The creatures were kept inside special containment units that resembled cages, but magical wards prevented them from escaping or hurting the visitors. This allowed the monster zoo to operate as a kind of sideshow attraction.

At some point, however, a major accident occurred. The magical wards wore off, freeing all of the monsters from their containment units. A mass evacuation began as everyone fled the zoo. To prevent the monsters from escaping into the surrounding world, magic was used to sink the entire monster zoo underground. The creatures became trapped beneath a massive amount of dirt, stone, and rubble.

Because the evacuation had happened so suddenly, the people running the zoo had not had time to retrieve the money they had collected that day. All of the monster zoo’s earnings were buried underground along with the monsters. The money had been insured by an insurance company, and as soon as I thought about that detail, I suddenly found myself inside an office.

Until that moment, I had been constructing the Storyverse world and its history myself with Lucid Light. Now, however, it was as though that history had always existed, and someone claiming to be my boss was explaining it to me. He was a bald man with a gray beard. He wore a blue business suit and a green striped tie. He told me about the monster zoo, its collapse, and the money still buried beneath it. Because the insurance company had insured the lost earnings, it might as well recover the actual money for itself. He wanted to send me to retrieve it because I was an adventurer who had dealt with dangerous situations like this before. I agreed to the assignment.

I traveled to the former site of the monster zoo, which was now covered by stone and rubble. Using my Lucid Light, I excavated an entrance into the buried structure. Beyond it was a deep, dark, cave-like tunnel that could still be filled with monsters. A long time had passed since the zoo’s collapse, so I reasoned that some of its creatures might already have died. Even so, it seemed foolish to run directly into the darkness and fight an unknown number of monsters. Instead, I devised a two-part plan.

For the first part of the plan, I stabbed Silonis—the Rainbow Crystal Sword, the Life Sword, and one of the most powerful artifacts in the Storyverse—into the ground. I sent a pulse of life energy through the earth and guided it until it located the treasure chest containing the monster zoo’s earnings. When the energy reached the chest, it brought the chest to life.

I could see through the living treasure chest’s eyes. Deep inside the buried zoo, the chest was being held by a dragon. At the same time, I could feel through my own body that the pulse of life energy had alerted a mole monster. The creature was burrowing through the ground toward the source of the pulse and coming directly for me. The dragon had also realized that the treasure chest it was holding had suddenly come alive. I was caught in a strange race against time: I needed to bring the chest back to me before the mole monster arrived, while also escaping before the dragon could do anything catastrophic.

I remembered an ability I had used in a recent dream, perhaps a couple of nights earlier, in which I fused my life energy with something else. Previously, I had used that ability to fuse with Silonis. This time, I wondered why I could not fuse with the living treasure chest instead.

Although the chest was deep inside the cave and still being held by the dragon, I activated the fusion. The process ripped the treasure chest completely out of the buried zoo and fused it into my body. I transformed into a combination of a man, wood, and gold. Because the fusion happened almost instantly, it effectively allowed me to teleport the entire chest directly to myself. I had not merely transported the treasure, however; I had become one with it.

That solved the problem of retrieving the chest, but the dragon was now searching for its missing treasure, and the mole monster was still burrowing toward me. I made my escape by backdashing away from the entrance with a burst of speed generated by my Lucid Light. I then cast a portal spell across the entrance to the cave.

The portal did not lead anywhere. Anything that attempted to pass through it would simply be sent back out through the same side it had entered. It effectively functioned as a wall—not because passage through it was impossible, but because no one could ever emerge on the opposite side. It was like a revolving door that could be entered endlessly without ever allowing anyone to reach the other side.

Once I was safely away, all I needed to do was cancel the life-energy fusion. The treasure chest and I divided back into two separate beings, leaving me with the recovered chest of gold to return to the insurance company. That was the end of the dream.
