The dream began with me sitting inside a sentient, wood-paneled jacuzzi in the middle of the Arctic. The jacuzzi had rainbow-colored RGB lights arranged like eyes and a mouth, almost like the face of a jack-o’-lantern, although there was nothing frightening about it. It was friendly and attentive, seemingly devoted to helping me relax and have a genuinely good time.

The setting created a pleasant contrast. The air outside was bitterly cold, but the water surrounding me was perfectly warm. I settled into the jacuzzi and tried to enjoy the peacefulness of the moment.

As I relaxed, however, I began thinking about how many of my dreams involved superhero adventures. I wondered whether simply sitting in a jacuzzi might become dull by comparison. The jacuzzi seemed to understand my concern. Hover pads activated beneath it, lifting us off the frozen ground and carrying us upward into the sky.

We flew directly into the Aurora Borealis.

I experienced a kind of beauty I had never seriously imagined before: the sensation of physically flying through the northern lights. The glowing clouds stretched through the sky in long, twisting tendrils that reminded me of blood vessels. Because the aurora was electromagnetic, I felt a gentle tingling sensation across my skin whenever we passed through it. The green and pink light broke apart around us like cotton candy, constantly shifting and reforming as the jacuzzi carried me through it.

While we flew, I began hearing orchestral music rising from somewhere below.

I looked down and saw a steampunk train racing across the landscape beneath us. It appeared to be some kind of war train involved in an ongoing battle. I could not tell who was fighting or what the conflict was about, but the train was clearly armed.

Without warning, it began firing at the jacuzzi.

I had no interest in becoming involved in their war, so I instructed the jacuzzi to fly higher. We climbed until we were safely beyond the train’s firing range.

A moment later, however, someone appeared in the sky behind us.

He resembled a cowboy riding a flying steampunk horse. He pursued us through the aurora and fired a musket in our direction. His shot struck both me and the jacuzzi.

I drew my rainbow crystal sword, the Sword of Light, and used it to create a glowing barrier around us. The barrier protected us from further musket fire while also healing the injuries that we had already sustained.

The cowboy continued chasing us. He had no intention of leaving us alone.

I summoned my golden energy tether and launched it toward the steampunk horse. The tether connected us and created a magical bond between me and the machine. Although the horse seemed to operate through gears, steam, and other analog mechanisms, the bond allowed me to interface with it as though I were hacking into a computer.

Through that connection, I activated the horse’s self-destruct system.

The cowboy’s pursuit came to an abrupt end, and the jacuzzi and I continued onward.

Eventually, we arrived at a living room floating freely in the sky. The room had an unusual design. Three of its walls were missing, leaving most of the room completely open to the clouds. A couch rested against the one solid back wall, and a television stood across from it, positioned so that someone could sit and watch it while surrounded by the open sky.

I climbed out of the jacuzzi and sat on the couch.

I began thinking about different things I could do to relax. Instead of watching television, I pulled out a notebook. It was made from purple felt and looked as though it had been stitched together from several soft, patchwork pieces.

I decided to draw.

I was not especially skilled at drawing in the waking world, but dreams did not have the same limitations. Inside a dream, I could draw anything I wanted. The idea felt novel to me. I did not normally spend my dreams doing something so quiet and personal.

I summoned a magical pencil. It glowed with a glittering pink light.

The moment I opened the notebook, I felt a wave of nostalgic warmth, as though I were sitting beside a fireplace somewhere safe and familiar.

I placed the pencil against the page and began drawing.

The image was not exactly a flower. It resembled a leafy green plant stalk, but instead of a blossom at the top, there was a radiant star. Bands of sunset colors spread outward from the star in a circular pattern, filling the scene around it.

Although I was drawing with a pencil, the finished image looked more like a watercolor painting with delicate ink and sketch details layered over it. I found it peaceful and deeply satisfying to look at.

There was something special about beginning with a blank page and then placing a small part of myself onto it. The drawing did not need to be shown to anyone else. It could exist entirely for me, and that alone gave it meaning.

As I continued thinking about the value of creating things purely for myself, a real plant began growing around my arms.

The vines curled and intertwined until they formed the shape of a guitar made from leaves, flowers, and living stems.

The sky around the floating living room changed. The Arctic aurora disappeared, replaced by a bright blue sky filled with soft, fluffy pink clouds.

I stood in the open room and began strumming the vine guitar.

As I played, I sang a song about creation and personal freedom. I sang about how other people might become angry or offended by the things someone chooses to make, but their anger does not give them ownership over another person’s imagination.

The song celebrated private creation as something rewarding in itself. Art did not need an audience to matter. Something could be intimate, personal, and meaningful even when it was created for only one person.

The hook of the song declared:

“Cry me a river, bleed me a lake.
You can’t take or control what I make.”

Standing among the blue sky and pink clouds, surrounded by the floating living room, the sentient jacuzzi, and the living guitar wrapped around my arms, I continued singing about the freedom to create whatever belonged to me.
