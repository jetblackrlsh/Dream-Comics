Dream Journal Reconstruction

I emerged from a dimensional elevator alongside a woman named Autumn. She had medium-length, curly brown hair and wore a great deal of makeup. Her Victorian-style dress was reddish brown and covered in golden leaf patterns, making her appearance perfectly suited to her name.

Autumn told me that she was my wife. This immediately seemed strange because I did not believe that I was married to her and had no memory of her at all. She found my memory problems annoying and said that we had already had conversations like this before. From her perspective, this was apparently not the first time I had forgotten her or our shared history.

The elevator behind us was not attached to a building. It stood by itself in the middle of a desert filled with rocks and sand. Enormous castles made from black bones rose across the landscape. Above us, the sky resembled a lava lamp: colorful blobs of molten material moved continuously around the planet, forming a thick barrier that concealed the actual sky.

I asked Autumn where we were. She explained that we had just traveled through the dimensional elevator and claimed that we did this all the time. I still could not remember Autumn, the elevator, or any of our supposed previous journeys together. When I asked what we were supposed to do next, she used her magic to summon the god Apollo and ask him for guidance.

The lava-filled sky opened only partially. Apollo descended through the opening in his chariot and asked what we wanted. We asked whether this world was a good place. Apollo said that it was not. As a god closely associated with the sun, he objected to the planet being completely cut off from sunlight by the lava surrounding it. He told us that if we wanted to improve the world, we would have to clear the lava from the sky by defeating Meltronome, the god of lava.

Apollo then departed. Although I remained confused about nearly everything that was happening, I concluded that I was apparently working with him. Autumn told me that I was known as the Doorman because I operated the dimensional elevator. According to her, traveling between dimensions and undertaking adventures like this was something I regularly did, even though I could not remember any of it.

After helping me summon Apollo and explaining my identity, Autumn disappeared from the dream. I did not remember seeing her leave. It was possible that she had jumped onto the back of Apollo’s chariot and departed with him, but I was not certain. Regardless, I continued the mission without her.

I approached one of the huge castles constructed from black bones. A gigantic animated dinosaur skeleton made from the same black material attacked me and tried to eat me. Being swallowed was not especially dangerous, however, because the creature had no stomach—only an empty rib cage. Once I was inside it, I used Lucid Light to create a set of controls that allowed me to pilot the skeletal dinosaur like a mech.

This drew the attention of the commander of the black-boned dinosaurs, a divine being whose name I could not remember precisely. I believed it might have been something like Metalon. He was the god of blades, and the black bones under his control were effectively blades. In a broader sense, bones themselves were like blades, which connected the skeletal creatures to his divine domain.

The god of blades asked what business had brought me to his territory. I told him that I intended to defeat Meltronome, the god of lava. At first, he assumed that I had come to kill all the gods and warned that I would have to get through him. I clarified that I had no intention of destroying the entire pantheon. I only wanted to remove Meltronome.

He then suspected that I planned to kill their prime deity and take control of the world myself. I told him that I did not want Meltronome’s place in the divine hierarchy. Once he understood that eliminating Meltronome would simply leave an open position into which he might rise, the god of blades became supportive of my mission. He allowed me to continue and gave me one of his blades, which was made from the same black-bone material as his castles and dinosaurs.

I was transported to an academy, where a long line of people waited to meet a massive, gray-skinned man wearing a business suit. I had apparently been sent there to negotiate with him. Because I carried the blade of a divine being, I was allowed to bypass the line.

The hulking man recognized the blade and understood that I had a connection to a god. He proposed that we make a deal. He would grant me incredible magical ability—effectively unlimited magical power—but the bargain required a heavy price. Rather than making me personally pay that price, he selected another person at the academy to bear it.

The man absorbed the chosen person’s power. As a result, his already large body became even bigger and more muscular, and he transformed into a humanoid rhinoceros crackling with lightning. The person whose abilities he took appeared to have possessed both rhinoceros and electrical powers. I did not know what the transformed man intended to do with his new body, but everyone else in the academy was terrified of him.

Their fear was initially confusing because many of them had been waiting in line to make a deal with him. I believed that they had hoped to receive the unlimited magical power he ultimately gave to me. If one of them had been chosen, that person would have gained his power rather than being left at the mercy of the dangerous lightning rhinoceros he became.

As I thought about the arrangement, I came to understand that the gray-skinned man was probably the god of deals. His divine power allowed him to create extraordinary bargains, transfers, and sacrifices, but it was bound by conditions. He could give someone unlimited magical power, yet that did not mean he could freely use unlimited power for himself.

The gods of this world appeared to be in constant competition and might even kill one another. A god whose abilities revolved around making deals would be vulnerable to more directly destructive deities such as the gods of blades and lava. The god of deals may therefore have wanted to surrender his divine status so that he would no longer be a target. However, he did not want to become a powerless, ordinary man in such a dangerous world.

His bargain solved both problems. He transferred the burden and danger of his godlike power to me, while the required sacrifice allowed him to take a new set of straightforward combat abilities for himself. From his perspective, becoming a powerful lightning rhinoceros may have been preferable to remaining a god with limitless but heavily conditional magic. He chose me because the black-bone blade demonstrated that I already had dealings with divine beings and might be capable of carrying what he wanted to relinquish.

Although I had originally come to confront Meltronome rather than the lightning rhinoceros, the transformed deal-maker was clearly a menace. The academy’s occupants were afraid that he might kill them all. I used some of my newly amplified magic to cast my portal spell, Porta. The spell divided his body spatially without cutting him apart or producing blood and gore. I sent his legs through a portal to some distant part of the desert, where they may have been left wandering by themselves, while his upper body remained trapped inside the academy classroom. He was greatly annoyed, but without his legs he could do little to hurt anyone.

The unlimited magical power I had received made the remainder of my mission much easier. The former god of deals had been restricted by bargains and sacrifices, but I had possessed magic long before meeting him. I had already experienced many Storyverse adventures, could wield Lucid Light, and could cast spells such as Porta without negotiating deals. Autumn also claimed that I had completed many forgotten dimensional adventures as the Doorman. Once his divine power became mine, I could channel it through magical abilities that did not share his original limitations.

Using that godlike power—possibly all of it—I cast Meltronome away. The lava that had surrounded the planet and filled its sky finally cleared. Sunlight returned to the world, and Apollo rose into the open sky like the sun itself. He declared himself the world’s new ultimate god.

With Meltronome defeated and Apollo installed above the world, I had completed my mission as the Doorman. I did not know why Apollo wanted to rule this particular planet, but its previous divine structure had been violent and predatory, so I hoped that he would make the world better.

I was left wondering whether I might return to my role in a future dream. Perhaps I would once again operate the dimensional elevator, reunite with Autumn, and travel with my mysterious wife to another world where we would find ourselves fighting gods together.
