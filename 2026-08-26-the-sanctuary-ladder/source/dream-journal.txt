Dream Journal Reconstruction
I induced lucidity through sleep paralysis and entered one of my spirit-hopping dreams—one of the dreams in which, for at least part of the experience, I am not myself.

At the beginning of the dream, I was lucid, but I was inhabiting the body of a black werewolf. I could tell immediately that this was not my normal body. Even stranger than what I was, however, was where I was.

I stood on top of a glowing expanse of liquid pink glass. It moved beneath me in waves, yet I could stand on its cold, smooth surface as though it possessed perfect surface tension. It was like walking on water, except that it also felt like solid glass. Although the surface itself was moving, it did not carry me with it. I remained completely still.

Oddly shaped black rocks protruded from the liquid glass around me. Above my head, something like a ceiling pulsed softly with pale blue light. It was not the sky, a skylight, an artificial ceiling, or even a recognizable light source. The entire enclosure felt organic, as though I were somewhere inside a living thing. Beautiful music filled the space without coming from any identifiable source. A woman's voice sang a song about sanctuary, even though I appeared to be completely alone.

The pink glow from the liquid glass was steady and relatively bright, though not blinding. The blue light above me was softer and pulsed gently. I could not clearly see the walls because they were so distant that they seemed to disappear into darkness, but I could sense the rounded shape of the enclosure. It felt somewhat like the inside of a huge, compressed sphere.

Once I had finished trying to understand the sanctuary, I began questioning why I was a black werewolf instead of myself. I recognized that this was another spirit-hopping dream and wondered whether I could learn why I kept inhabiting other beings. I summoned the rainbow crystal sword into my hands and was pleased that I could still call it to me despite being in someone else's body. I had done something similar a couple of nights earlier, when I had also been someone else but could still summon the sword.

I asked the rainbow crystal sword why I was this other being rather than myself. The sword answered that the werewolf was simply another one of its holders from across time. I remembered an older dream in which I jumped between different holders of the sword throughout history. Many of those holders had been women, although not all of the people I become during spirit-hopping dreams are women. The sword had also belonged to men, and apparently this black werewolf—who had occupied this peaceful but surreal sanctuary—was another person in that lineage.

The next thing I knew, I was climbing down a ladder suspended high in the sky. Above me floated an enormous black rock. It was smooth and shaped somewhat like a flattened sphere. I initially compared its size to a football stadium, although it was probably much larger. I could not tell exactly how the ladder connected to it, but I understood that the immense floating rock was the sanctuary I had just left.

The ladder extended so far above the world that I could not understand how I was expected to climb either up or down it. Looking around, I noticed other floating rocks and rocky islands, as though this were a place where huge pieces of land naturally hovered in the sky. Far below me was what appeared to be a normal city, but it was incredibly distant from my position on the ladder.

Suddenly, the ladder began extending rapidly toward the ground while I held on. It continued until it reached the city below. Once the ladder connected with the ground, the dream transitioned into a non-lucid state.

I found myself inside an academy building, now inhabiting my own body again. Many people I remembered from school were present, although I did not focus on anyone by name. There was some kind of video that we were all supposed to be watching, but almost no one was paying attention to it.

People asked me questions about my gadgets. I had my smartphone mounted in a black holder on my wrist, as I often do in waking life. It connected through a yellow cord to the large blue augmented-reality glasses that resembled sunglasses. The glasses allowed me to see my phone's screen floating before me like a hologram.

I talked with people around the academy and noticed a display case containing interesting action figures. I was surprised to see the display. I also discovered a message from a friend who was disappointed that I had not read it because they had wanted to do something big for their birthday. I apologized and explained that I was simply very bad at checking my messages, then tried to comfort them. There were also various women around the academy.

At some point, I experienced a false awakening. I woke in a bed inside the academy. The room was now dark, and the woman who had been showing everyone the video was still sitting there watching it by herself. She was illuminated by the television's blue light.

I gathered my belongings, including scattered clothes and some action figures, put everything into a bag, and hurried out of the academy. As I tried to leave, the woman turned off the television. The blue light vanished, plunging the entire space into darkness.

When I emerged from that darkness, I was still inside the academy, but the environment was bright again. I was no longer in the television-viewing room. Instead, I moved through an attractive art installation. What appeared to be colorful gemstones hung in elaborate patterns in the air, suspended by black metal bars. Upon looking more closely, I realized that the colorful objects were not gemstones at all. They were pieces of insulation.

I became concerned that it might not be healthy to stand near the installation. I did not know whether the insulation contained fiberglass or something even more dangerous, such as asbestos. The beauty of the artwork began to seem deceptive.

I noticed a large window, beyond which several female artists were talking with one another. Something about them felt deeply off-putting, as though they radiated a fiery red aura. I began to suspect that the potential danger of the art installation was intentional and that none of these people could be trusted.

I tried to make a break for it. As I ran, I passed one man wearing a green shirt, gray shorts, and a white baseball cap. Everyone else I passed was a woman. The women began screeching and shouting, "Man! It's a man!" They chased me while brandishing utensils and tried to stab me with them.

I could not understand why they were targeting me when the man in the baseball cap was standing right there. I wondered whether I had entered a world where women ate men, although that still did not explain why they ignored him. I did not want him to be eaten, but I could not determine the rule separating us. I wondered whether they were targeting me because I was Black, whether this was a world in which only one man was permitted to exist, or whether some completely different rule was at work.

I escaped from the mob and entered a stairwell, shutting the door behind me. The women did not follow me inside. Ahead of me, a woman with a black ponytail, a black-and-white striped shirt, wide hips, and a black skirt hurried up the stairs. Unlike the other women, she did not chase or attack me. Instead, she seemed to be deliberately avoiding me.

With nowhere else to go, I followed her upward. I wanted to stop her long enough to ask questions and understand what was happening, so I attempted to restrain her with Lucid Light. The instant I realized that I could use my dream power, I thought, "Wait a minute—I have Lucid Light?" Remembering the power made me realize that I was dreaming, restoring my lucidity.

As soon as I became lucid again, I found myself back on the ladder from the beginning of the dream. I was no longer the black werewolf. This time, I inhabited the body of a humanoid fire elemental made of red and yellow flame. I clung to the ladder high above the city, but the ladder was no longer connected to the ground.

Looking back on everything, I understood the sanctuary and the floating landscape as a kind of lucid-dream space. It was a realm in which I could recognize that I was dreaming, even if spirit hopping meant that I was not always permitted to inhabit my own body. The city below represented the grounded, non-lucid dream. When the ladder extended down and touched the city, that connection grounded me into the non-lucid academy experience and disconnected me from my awareness. When Lucid Light restored my lucidity, I returned to the elevated ladder after it had separated from the ground.

The connection between the academy and my new fiery body also stood out to me. The threatening women had seemed to radiate fiery red energy, and when I returned to the lucid realm, I had become a red-and-yellow fire elemental. Whatever the exact relationship was, the sanctuary, the ladder, the grounded city, and my shifting bodies all seemed to reflect the movement between lucid awareness and an ordinary dream state.
