# Page Script — The Director of Agentis

All story text appears inside unnumbered rectangular caption boxes generated directly in the art. No speech bubbles, thought bubbles, floating dialogue, subtitles, watermarks, logos, numbered captions, or issue numbers. Every page is a complete 4:5 portrait anime comic page. Jet's business-suit appearance, Mara, the Agentis insignia, and the command center architecture remain consistent.

## Page 1 — Cover

Jet stands on the central director's platform in his black suit, looking over an immense circular command center filled with humanlike AI agents at cyan workstations. Mara stands beside him with her translucent tablet. Behind them, a window shows glowing Storyverse bubble universes connected like a digital constellation. Visible cover text only: “THE DIRECTOR OF AGENTIS” and “JULY 13, 2026”. No issue number.

## Page 2 — Lucid Arrival

Four panels. Jet emerges from a shadowy sleep-paralysis threshold into a brilliant futuristic corridor; he discovers he is wearing a business suit; Mara approaches and gestures forward; enormous doors open onto cyan light.

Caption text:

- “I induced lucidity through sleep paralysis and arrived alone in a futuristic corridor.”
- “Instead of my usual hoodie and Dream Anime shirt, I wore a business suit tailored as if someone expected me.”
- “A woman greeted me with the confidence of someone who already knew my name and my destination.”
- “She ushered me toward the command center of an organization called Agentis.”

## Page 3 — The Director

Four panels. Mara escorts Jet onto the central platform; hundreds of diverse agents turn attentively toward him; Mara presents him as director; Jet leans toward her with a skeptical, amused question while she answers with dry patience.

Caption text:

- “The command center was filled with people, screens, and workstations arranged around a platform waiting for its leader.”
- “My guide identified me as the director of the entire organization.”
- “I asked the obvious question: Was I the head of a spy organization?”
- “She answered, ‘Not every organization of agents is an organization of secret agents.’”

## Page 4 — The Mirror

Five panels. A cyan interface over one workstation shows comic thumbnails, research notes, editing marks, code, and a publishing arrow; Jet recognizes the pattern; different agents appear as designer, researcher, editor, engineer, and coordinator; the full room becomes a cyberspace mirror of Jet working at a computer; Jet looks amazed and uneasy.

Caption text:

- “Then I understood what kind of agents they were: AI agents, visualized as actual people.”
- “One researched. One designed. One edited. Another prepared the result for publication.”
- “Agentis reflected my waking habit of using AI agents to help me get things done, especially to make comics.”
- “It was the cyberspace equivalent of what I essentially do every day.”
- “The familiar looked completely different when every tool stood in front of me as a person waiting for direction.”

## Page 5 — Too Many Waiting

Four panels. Jet stands small before rows of attentive agents; multiple holographic task paths branch in all directions; Jet tries to address several teams at once and becomes visually overwhelmed; the panels quiet as he admits uncertainty to Mara.

Caption text:

- “I still did not really know what Agentis did, because I had more available agents than defined jobs.”
- “When they were only windows on a screen, I never had to picture all of them waiting for the next command.”
- “Now it was obvious that I was not accustomed to using so many agents in parallel.”
- “Having enormous capacity was not the same as knowing what I wanted that capacity to become.”

## Page 6 — The Company Question

Four panels. Holograms depict many solo creators running tiny agent-powered companies; Jet views his existing comics website with a support-heart icon; a grand corporate tower blueprint appears and Jet pushes it aside; he studies a looser constellation of his different media forms.

Caption text:

- “Many people are becoming solopreneurs, using AI agents in place of employees to build companies.”
- “I already published comics on a site with support options for people who wanted to donate.”
- “I had not reached that point, and I did not know what I would do differently if I called my work a company.”
- “Beyond that, I could imagine one place that brought more of my media landscape together—but I needed more time to think.”

## Page 7 — One Honest Assignment

Five panels. Jet closes the corporate blueprint; he calmly divides the crowd into three small teams; one team catalogs existing comics, one maps the website and support path, one sketches connections to other media; Mara smiles as the noisy interfaces simplify; the teams collaboratively build a single luminous constellation rather than a corporate empire.

Caption text:

- “I did not need to invent a company merely because the command center made one possible.”
- “I gave Agentis one honest assignment: map what I already make before deciding what else it should become.”
- “One team organized the comics. One studied the existing site and its support options.”
- “A third sketched ways that my other media might connect without forcing a final answer.”
- “For the first time, parallel agents felt less like a crowd waiting on me and more like collaborators exploring a clear question.”

## Page 8 — A Different View

Four panels. The organized media constellation floats above the central platform; agents work calmly in a few teams while others relax in a comfortable standby area; Jet and Mara look through the tall window where digital connections turn into Storyverse bubble universes; final wide panel shows Jet reflected in the glass, business suit visually overlapping his familiar hoodie silhouette, smiling thoughtfully.

Caption text:

- “The command center had not needed a grand purpose. It needed a direction small enough to begin.”
- “My comics, support options, and possible future media were the same as before, but now I could see their relationships.”
- “I still did not know exactly what Agentis would become, and I did not have to decide it all at once.”
- “It is bizarre how different your current circumstances can appear when you choose to look at them differently.”
