# Treatment - The Sky-Iron Frog

## Premise

In a radiant fairy village grown among magical trees, Jet faces a giant frog kaiju whose black sky-iron samurai armor makes ordinary spells useless. A failed attempt to remove that armor gives Jet the conceptual breakthrough needed to reorganize the fairies and save their home.

## Central conflict and theme

The immediate conflict is clear: the fairies cannot stop an armored predator that nullifies magic, and Jet cannot detach the armor because the frog's mucus adheres like supernatural glue. The deeper conflict is one of classification. Jet has assumed that every impossible power in a magical world must be magic. By paying attention to contradictory evidence, he discovers that different names can reveal different rules. The story's theme is that a failed solution can become valuable evidence when the hero is willing to question an old assumption.

## Tone and visual direction

The issue is a bright, high-saturation modern-anime fantasy kaiju adventure. The village glows with emerald, cyan, violet, amber, and magenta light. Treehouse homes are organically grown into enormous enchanted trunks. Fairy powers appear as distinct visual languages: conventional magic forms ornate runes and sparkling spellwork, while psychic attacks emerge as concentrated fire, ice, and lightning shaped by focused will. The frog's wet green skin and black samurai armor give the threat imposing weight without gore.

## Story structure

Jet arrives in the fairy village and studies its different magical trees, homes, fairy colors, and abilities. A huge humanoid frog attacks with a long tongue, but the fairies combine their powers and drive it away before Jet needs to intervene.

The frog returns wearing black samurai armor. This time the fairies' spells dissolve harmlessly against the dark plates. Jet recalls legends of meteorite ore called sky iron and concludes that the armor may suppress magic.

Jet uses Geomancy to pull on the mineral ore within the armor. The plates move, proving that he can affect them, but a powerful mucus membrane glues the armor to the frog. Jet cannot remove it, and the village appears doomed.

Then Jet recognizes the contradiction: anti-magic armor should have blocked a magical Geomancy spell, yet his power still moved the metal. He compares magical elemental control with psychic abilities such as pyrokinesis and realizes that his Geomancy may actually be Geokinesis. The same distinction may apply to some fairies.

Jet orders the magical fairies to retreat and calls every psychic fairy to the front. A pyrokinetic fairy burns through the frog's defenses, a cryokinetic fairy locks its movement in ice, and an electrokinetic fairy blasts it away with lightning. Their focused psychic attacks bypass the sky iron because they are not magic.

## Ending and emotional arc

The village survives, and its fairies celebrate beneath their glowing treehouses. Jet does not claim absolute certainty about the nature of dream powers. Instead, he looks into the Storyverse sky and considers a new theory: because dreams are experiences of the mind, perhaps every power he has called magic is ultimately psychic. The visual ending turns victory into curiosity, showing that the most important thing Jet defeated was not only the frog, but an assumption.
