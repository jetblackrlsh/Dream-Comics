Dream Journal Reconstruction
Before I went to bed, I had been thinking about two character concepts: Kaiju and Kindle. Those ideas followed me into sleep. I often experience dreams in which I am the main character going on an adventure, but this was one of the other kinds of lucid dreams I sometimes have. Instead of placing myself directly in the story, I used my Lucid Light to create a new world in the Storyverse. I established the initial conditions of that universe and then watched it from a distance as a creator and observer, seeing what developed around the characters I had imagined.

The first character was Kaiju, a hulking, monstrously muscular superhero with orange skin, red eyes, and short, messy brown hair. His hair was almost shaped like a bowl cut, although it swept more to one side. He wore purple shorts. Despite his intimidating appearance, Kaiju was extremely intelligent, kindhearted, and committed to using his powers to help people rather than hurt them. Unfortunately, his enormous muscles, orange skin, and red eyes made him look like a monster even when he was standing at an ordinary human height. People were frequently frightened of him, and shrinking himself did not entirely solve that problem because he remained disproportionately muscular and intimidating at every scale.

Kaiju possessed several interconnected transformation abilities. He could manipulate his size, becoming extremely small or growing into an immense giant like the kaiju for which he was named. He could also transform into humanoid versions of animals and even mythical creatures. Whatever creature form he adopted retained his orange coloration, red eyes, and massive musculature. In addition, Kaiju could roboticize his current form. Silver metal plating would form across his body, but this was not merely armor. His body truly changed from an organic being into a mechanical one. If he roboticized himself while transformed into another creature, he became a robotic version of that creature.

Because Kaiju frightened so many people despite his gentle nature, he had built a humanoid robot named Kindle to be both his crime-fighting partner and his romantic partner. Kindle had a predominantly white, black, and orange color scheme. In place of ordinary hair, she had two large black rabbit ears that hung behind her head and were tied back together almost like a ponytail. Her face was a black LCD screen displaying two very large, perfectly round orange eyes that resembled glowing zeroes. Her design was distinctly robotic, but it was also expressive and immediately recognizable.

The world that grew around Kaiju and Kindle placed them in San Francisco. I did not initially remember the city because my attention was centered much more strongly on the characters than on the setting, but San Francisco became important once the story unfolded. Kaiju served as one of the city’s heroes, although he was not the sort of superhero normally summoned to stop a bank robbery or confront ordinary criminals. His powers and experience were best suited to battling genuine giant monsters—enormous creatures capable of destroying buildings and killing people. I watched him defend San Francisco against multiple giant monster threats, using his different sizes and transformations to meet each creature on its own scale.

Then a nuclear bomb came falling out of the sky toward San Francisco. Kaiju had to find a way to stop it before it could reach the city. He expanded into his giant form and transformed into a huge, muscular, humanoid orange dragon. Ordinary fire breath would not have been powerful enough to detonate or destroy the bomb safely, so he roboticized his dragon body. Silver metal plating spread across his immense form as he became a giant robotic dragon. From that form, he unleashed a nuclear fire-breath beam into the sky. The beam struck the falling weapon and caused it to detonate while it was still far enough away that the explosion did not wipe out San Francisco.

News reports revealed that the bomb had been launched by a country called Anti-Tekistan. Anti-Tekistan was a nation deeply opposed to modern technology and especially fearful of artificial intelligence. It had targeted San Francisco because the city was a major hub for American AI companies, and the attack was intended to destroy a significant part of the country’s AI infrastructure.

Just when it appeared that Kaiju had saved the city, a vast force of paramilitary soldiers working for Anti-Tekistan flooded into the area. The nuclear strike had apparently been their first plan, while the ground assault was a second plan meant to continue the mission if the bomb failed. The soldiers did not carry ordinary firearms. Their rifles fired nanites that forced advanced technology to devolve into analog equivalents. Mobile phones struck by the nanites transformed into rotary phones. Electric cars became Model Ts. Although the nanites were themselves an advanced technology, they were being used to strip digital and electronic technology from everything they touched. The paramilitary force intended to move through San Francisco, destroy its technological infrastructure, and eventually reach the AI companies at the center of their campaign.

Kaiju recognized that the nanites posed a uniquely dangerous threat to him. He shrank from his giant robotic dragon form back to his ordinary human scale, although he remained huge and muscular relative to everyone around him. He also ended his roboticization. He had no idea what the nanites might turn him into if they struck him while his body was mechanical, and he did not want to discover what an analog equivalent of his robotic form might be.

He began fighting the paramilitary soldiers while trying desperately not to kill any of them. Against giant monsters, Kaiju was willing to use lethal force when necessary to prevent them from destroying buildings and harming people. Human opponents were different. He was not accustomed to fighting ordinary people, and he was genuinely afraid that his overwhelming strength might kill them. He clapped his hands together to create shockwaves that hurled soldiers backward and knocked some of them unconscious. He repeatedly shifted into different humanoid creature forms, searching for some transformation that would let him control the enormous crowd without seriously injuring anyone.

The problem was not that any of the soldiers were individually stronger than Kaiju. There were simply far too many of them for him to handle cautiously. Every time he restrained himself to avoid killing someone, more soldiers advanced. He continued changing forms and experimenting with nonlethal ways to stop them, but his compassion was causing him to lose ground. The enormous paramilitary force gradually surrounded and overwhelmed him in a flood of bodies. It began to look as though even his tremendous power would not be enough to protect the city without violating the moral limits he had placed upon himself.

That was when Kindle discovered that he was in danger. She could connect remotely with electronic devices and networks, and she accessed the city’s camera grid to see what was happening to Kaiju. She immediately flew toward him using the propulsion systems built into her feet. Her feet were not shaped like jet engines; they functioned more like hover boots, allowing her to rise smoothly through the air and travel above the city.

Kindle’s arrival placed her in exceptional danger because she was entirely robotic. Neither she nor Kaiju knew what the nanites would do to her if they forced her advanced mechanical body into some analog equivalent. Kaiju warned her not to come too close to the soldiers or place herself within range of their rifles.

Instead of retreating, Kindle increased her altitude. As she climbed higher into the sky, she used her camera systems to calculate the soldiers’ firing angles and the maximum distance their nanite rounds could travel. She determined the precise altitude at which she would be too high for any of their shots to reach her. Once she was safely beyond the rifles’ effective range, she turned her attention away from the soldiers themselves and toward the technology inside their weapons.

Using her ability to connect remotely with other devices, Kindle established a connection with the nanites contained inside every rifle. She then deactivated them all at once. Across the battlefield, the Anti-Tekistan soldiers’ weapons became inert. Without functioning nanites, they could no longer devolve the city’s technology, threaten Kindle’s robotic body, or carry out their attack against San Francisco’s AI infrastructure.

The paramilitary soldiers were effectively defeated. Although many of them were still physically present, they had not brought conventional guns, and their ground mission had primarily been designed to destroy advanced technology rather than directly hurt people. Their first plan would have killed an enormous number of people with a nuclear explosion, and their second plan had initially made frightening progress, but neither plan succeeded. Kaiju had stopped the catastrophic weapon and held back the invading force without abandoning his refusal to kill human beings. Kindle had then found the precise, intelligent, nonviolent solution that his physical strength alone could not provide. Together, the heroic partners saved San Francisco and protected its people and technology from Anti-Tekistan.
