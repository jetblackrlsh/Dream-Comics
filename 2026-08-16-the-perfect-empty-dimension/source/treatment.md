# Treatment - The Perfect Empty Dimension

## Logline

After a reversible rescue sends a slime monster into a dimension of absolute solitude, Jet enters the same realm and discovers a perfect life without judgment—only to face a version of himself who wants him to abandon the waking world forever.

## Premise

Jet begins the dream flying through a modern city like a superhero. He passes a gigantic green slime monster because it appears harmless, but the creature theatrically attacks the street to demand his attention. When Jet calls its behavior performative, the monster reveals an adult woman trapped inside its tentacle.

Jet draws Silonis, the Rainbow Crystal Sword of Life. Rather than kill the captor or risk injuring the hostage, he fuses their life energy into a single gigantic green slime woman. The fusion removes the immediate power imbalance, but both beings are horrified by the loss of bodily identity. Jet immediately recognizes that the fusion can only be temporary, separates them with Silonis, and restores each to an independent body.

## Central conflict and theme

Jet casts Porta and sends the slime monster to an Empty Dimension rather than the Glimmer, the spirit-prison he discovered for dangerous magical beings. The Empty Dimension is not torture; it is a place where the creature can be alone with its thoughts.

That explanation changes the conflict. Jet values solitude, self-narration, and freedom from judgment, so the supposed punishment begins to sound like refuge. He enters the dimension and divides into two selves: an observer who retains awareness of the waking world, and a resident who experiences the realm as ordinary life.

The theme is that authentic acceptance matters, but comfort without responsibility can become a seductive form of surrender. Jet does not reject the perfect world because it is secretly evil. He rejects permanence because his real life still contains chosen commitments worth returning to.

## Escalation

The resident self lives among familiar people in a softly digital neighborhood. Jet follows his unconventional routines—eating once a day, sleeping on the floor, and living according to his own preferences—without ridicule or interrogation. The freedom is quieter than superhero power and, for that reason, more personally tempting.

After what feels like a long time, observer-Jet realizes it is nearly eight in the morning. Resident-Jet speaks to him like a siren, arguing that this is a perfect world where they can remain themselves and never worry again.

## Ending and emotional arc

Jet acknowledges the offer without being deceived by it. He answers that he is good and still has things to do. The final page shows the perfect digital dimension fading as Jet deliberately returns to waking life. The emotional arc moves from quick heroic judgment, to respect for bodily autonomy, to the recognition that being accepted is precious, and finally to the choice that freedom also includes voluntarily honoring one's responsibilities.
