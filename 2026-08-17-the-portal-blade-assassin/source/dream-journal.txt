Dream Journal Reconstruction

Unlike many of my dreams, this one did not begin with me flying through a city like a superhero. Instead, I found myself inside a magical study, thinking deeply about magical swords and the underlying rules of the Storyverse.

I was considering how Silonis, the Rainbow Crystal Sword of Life, is regarded as one of the most powerful swords in the Storyverse. Savannah possesses the Death Sword, and the Life Sword and Death Sword have both been described as being among the most powerful weapons in existence. However, it seemed as though there could easily be many other kinds of blades of power that I had never fully considered before.

My Porta spell was one example of another tremendously powerful form of magic. Porta allowed me to travel almost instantly to nearly anywhere in the Storyverse, and I had recently begun using it against opponents. I could send dangerous magical beings into the Glimmer, a prison dimension in which they existed as spirits and could no longer hurt anyone. Alternatively, I could send someone into the Empty Dimension, which was more peaceful and gave its inhabitants the freedom and solitude to reflect on their thoughts. If Porta was powerful enough to accomplish things like that, I wondered whether magical swords could also be created to wield the same kind of portal magic.

As I thought about this possibility, I began developing a clearer understanding of the different kinds of magical swords that could exist throughout the Storyverse. Part of what made Silonis so powerful was that it was not merely a normal sword carrying a magical enchantment. It was the Rainbow Crystal Life Sword, constructed through a combination of futuristic technology and special living crystals capable of manipulating life energy. The union of that advanced technology with the extraordinary properties of the crystals was what elevated Silonis beyond an ordinary enchanted weapon.

Even so, I could easily imagine someone creating a more conventional life sword. Porta was a spell that could be cast to open a portal, and it seemed likely that Viva and Vita were life spells capable of producing forms of life-energy manipulation. If those spells could be cast normally, they could probably also be inscribed into objects. Someone with the necessary mystical knowledge could place a life spell onto a normal sword and create a genuine life sword. The only thing preventing people from doing so might be that they did not know it was possible. Such a weapon would still lack the futuristic technology and specialized magical crystals that made the Rainbow Crystal Life Sword and the Death Sword so exceptional, but it would nevertheless qualify as a blade imbued with the power of life.

That led me back to the idea of a portal blade. A sword with an inscription of Porta could presumably channel portal magic, but I wondered what would be required to create a portal sword comparable in power and sophistication to Silonis. For that, there would need to be magical crystals whose natural property was opening portals.

I concluded that somewhere in the Storyverse, there was probably an entire world composed of portal crystals. It would likely be one of the most dangerous worlds in existence. Countless civilizations would need those crystals to construct teleportation technology. Rather than relying upon a person to cast Porta, engineers could combine the crystals with advanced machinery and automate their power. The resulting technology might include massive teleportation stations used for shipping materials across enormous distances, as well as small personal teleportation devices.

That seemed consistent with how something like Silonis could have been created in the first place. Magic already existed, but combining it with technology allowed its effects to be controlled, automated, and incorporated into machines.

Mining the portal-crystal world would be extraordinarily hazardous because the crystals would naturally open portals. Anything from anywhere in the Storyverse could suddenly emerge there without warning. Miners would need constant protection while extracting the gems, and heroes would likely be stationed across the planet to defend them from whatever creatures, people, objects, or other phenomena came through the spontaneously forming portals.

I sat in the magical study writing these discoveries down, fascinated by how many aspects of the Storyverse I had never considered before. As I recorded my ideas, however, an unseen adversary suddenly struck me. Something was appearing beside me with incredible speed, slicing me, and disappearing just as rapidly.

Every time I was struck in the dream, I could feel my physical body enter a coughing fit. After more than a year of constant lucid-dreaming practice, I had become skilled at maintaining my dream state while remaining partially aware of events occurring in the waking world. I could simultaneously feel myself being sliced inside the dream and coughing outside of it. The attacks and the coughing seemed to form a back-and-forth rhythm between the two realities.

At first, I did not know how to defend myself. My attacker appeared and vanished so quickly that I could not react in time. Then I realized that I did not need to see the attacker or match their speed. Using Silonis and its command over life energy, I sensed the life energy of the person who kept materializing, striking me, and disappearing. Once I could feel their presence, I knew where they were going to emerge before they completed their next attack.

I began parrying every strike with Silonis. Each time my adversary appeared, the Rainbow Crystal Sword was already in position to meet their blade. Although I could successfully stop the attacks, the enemy continued to vanish almost immediately after every attempted strike.

I realized that this person was wielding one of the portal-crystal swords I had just been contemplating. Their ability to appear, attack, and disappear was coming from the blade itself.

I then wondered whether portal swords might connect more than physical locations. Because their very nature involved connecting one thing to another, perhaps they could detect when someone began forming a connection with them even on an intellectual level. Becoming aware of the existence and nature of portal swords might itself alert whoever possessed them.

That possibility led me to an even larger realization. Perhaps a hidden clan of portal-blade assassins was deliberately hoarding the secret of these weapons. Whenever someone independently discovered the truth about portal swords, one of the assassins would sense that connection and eliminate the person before the knowledge could spread. That would explain why portal swords were not common throughout the Storyverse. If portal gems were more plentiful than life crystals, people should have been using portal swords everywhere. A secretive clan killing anyone who uncovered their existence would account for why I had never encountered them before.

The portal-blade assassin continued attacking, and I continued sensing their life energy and parrying every strike. Eventually, I decided to use portal magic of my own. I cast Porta and opened a passage into the Empty Dimension, banishing the assassin there.

I knew that someone carrying a portal-crystal sword should have been capable of opening another portal and returning immediately. Nevertheless, I felt confident that the assassin would choose not to come back.

The Empty Dimension was not a hellish prison of loneliness. When I had experienced it, it was a place of genuine freedom where a person could exist as themselves without judgment. My own existence was divided between two realities: I had the waking world, which still mattered to me and contained responsibilities I had to return to, and I had the Storyverse, which I also cared about deeply. I could not remain in the Empty Dimension forever because I eventually needed to awaken.

The portal-blade assassin did not share that dual existence. If someone lived only within the Storyverse and was sent into the Empty Dimension, they would not be choosing between two meaningful worlds. They might instead feel that they had finally discovered the perfect one. Being sent there could feel like finding heaven on Earth—a place so free and peaceful that there was no reason to leave.

I believed the assassin was now content within a private world of complete freedom. They had the power to return, but no desire to use it. I no longer needed to fight them, and they no longer needed to bother me.

That was how the dream ended.
