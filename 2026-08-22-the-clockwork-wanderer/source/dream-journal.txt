Dream Journal Reconstruction

I found myself exploring a clockwork planet that had formed from old spacecraft constructed during the analog era of space travel. Over time, these abandoned ships had drifted into one another and been drawn together by gravity until they formed an entire planetary body. The world beneath me was therefore composed of the remains of countless ancient vessels, with enormous gears and other mechanical structures covering much of its surface. Bronze, gold, and black dominated the planet’s color scheme, giving everything the appearance of a vast, antiquated machine assembled from the wreckage of a forgotten age.

As I explored this strange mechanical world, I encountered a woman wearing an unusual astronaut suit. Unlike the bulky, fabric-like suits that astronauts often wore, hers was extremely slim and fitted closely to her body. It looked almost like armor because it was solid rather than puffy, although it did not appear to be made of metal. It seemed to consist of some kind of hard white plastic polymer. I could tell that she was probably a woman from the shape of her body, but I could not see her face at all. Her astronaut helmet seemed to have a two-way mirrored visor that was completely opaque from the outside.

Glowing green orbs resembling globules of slime were positioned along the arms and legs of her suit. I did not know what they did. They might have been battery packs, they might have powered some of her equipment, or they might have had an entirely different function. Around the wrists of the suit, she wore cobalt-blue gauntlets formed from ring-like structures.

When I found her, she was actively fighting a group of barbarian women. The barbarians had pink skin, red eyes, and white hair, and they carried laser blasters that they were firing at the astronaut woman. They were also extremely muscular and possessed tremendous superhuman strength. Even the way they moved demonstrated how powerful they were, because their feet cracked the clockwork ground beneath them as they walked.

At first, the astronaut woman appeared to be badly outmatched. She did not have a laser blaster or any other obvious weapon of her own. However, the cobalt-blue gauntlets built into her suit could deflect the barbarians’ laser blasts and send them flying back toward their attackers. She reflected several shots into the group and knocked a couple of the barbarians down. The remaining women realized that they might also be struck by their own weapons, so they fled. They escaped by launching themselves into enormous superhuman leaps that carried them far away from the astronaut woman.

Although she had successfully driven them off, the fight still seemed incredibly dangerous for her. Her gauntlets allowed her to counter their ranged attacks, but I had the impression that if one of the barbarians managed to strike her directly with a punch, it might be over immediately. Despite its armored appearance, her suit seemed to be made from hard plastic rather than metal, and one of those tremendously strong women might have been able to cave in her helmet.

Once the barbarians were gone, I approached the astronaut woman and asked what she was searching for on the planet.

“The same thing everyone else is after,” she told me. “Treasure.”

She explained that many of the ancient clockwork vessels comprising the planet had once been space-pirate ships. Their wreckage still contained treasure chests filled with old credits, along with valuable antiques from the analog era of space travel. An enormous amount of abandoned wealth was hidden throughout the planet, and treasure hunters from across the galaxy wanted to claim it.

I asked whether the barbarian women had also come looking for treasure, and she reminded me that everyone on the planet was there for the same reason. I then assumed that she and the barbarians had been fighting because they refused to divide the treasure among themselves.

“No,” she corrected me. “I’m fighting them because they stole my ship.”

Without her spacecraft, she had no way to leave the clockwork planet. That made her situation seem even worse than I had originally realized. She was trapped on a hostile world populated by rival treasure hunters, including superhuman barbarians, and she had no obvious offensive weapons. Her gauntlets could turn laser fire back upon her attackers, but their effectiveness depended on someone else shooting at her.

I told her that I knew portal magic and could help her escape from the planet. She refused my assistance. She did not want a handout and insisted that she would be fine on her own, although she appreciated my offer.

Her response surprised me. From my perspective, she was in a terrible situation, yet she appeared largely unbothered by it. It was possible that the mysterious glowing green orbs powered her gauntlets or that her suit possessed offensive equipment I had not yet seen, but I had no way of knowing. She simply carried herself like someone who neither needed nor wanted anyone else to rescue her.

I respected her refusal, but I still knew of something that could make her situation less dangerous. I told her that I understood why she did not want to accept the portal, but that I could give her something else that might help. I used my Lucid Light to create a jetpack for her. The jetpack would allow her to travel rapidly around the planet, keep pace with the super-jumping barbarians, and dodge their attacks before they could get close enough to crush her helmet. Even if she never recovered her stolen ship, it would also give her the ability to fly away from the planet if she truly needed to escape.

The astronaut woman accepted the jetpack. When she activated it, wings unfolded from its sides, and she lifted into the air. She thanked me for giving it to her and then flew away over the horizon.

Unlike some of my other adventures, in which I encountered someone pursuing a mission and joined them for the rest of their journey, this meeting did not become a prolonged team-up. The astronaut woman was a lone-wolf kind of hero who intended to face her problems independently. She appreciated the jetpack I gave her, but she did not ask me to accompany her or help recover her ship. Our brief encounter ended as she disappeared beyond the horizon and continued her adventure on her own.
