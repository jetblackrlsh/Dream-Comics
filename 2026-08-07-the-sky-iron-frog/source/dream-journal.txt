Dream Journal Reconstruction

I was inside a world full of colorful fairies with different powers. The fairies lived together in harmony in a village made up of treehouse-like homes built among different kinds of magical trees. As I looked around, I noticed that the fairies came in different colors and that the different colors seemed to correspond to different types of magical abilities.

A giant humanoid frog suddenly appeared. It was essentially a giant humanoid frog kaiju, and it used its long tongue to snatch fairies out of the air as if they were flies so that it could eat them. The fairies quickly fought back with their powers and successfully defended themselves. The frog ran away, and the conflict ended so quickly that I did not really have to intervene.

Afterward, I took some time to explore the fairy village. I looked at the different magical trees that contained the treehouses and continued observing the different types of fairies and their powers.

Eventually, the giant humanoid frog kaiju returned. This time, however, it was wearing black armor that looked almost like samurai armor. When the fairies attacked it again, their magical powers did not seem to affect it at all. The frog began using its tongue to capture and eat fairies while their attacks failed against its armor.

I started thinking about what the armor might be made of. I remembered reading that stones from meteorites are sometimes described as having anti-magic properties and are sometimes called sky iron. I wondered whether the frog's armor might be made of sky iron. If that was the case, I figured that all I needed to do was remove the armor so that the fairies could defeat it normally.

One of the earliest powers I learned in the Storyverse was Geomancy. I reasoned that if the armor was made from metal containing stone or mineral ore, I should be able to manipulate the ore inside it with Geomancy. I used Geomancy on the armor and tried to pull it away from the frog's body. The armor moved a little, but the frog had an extremely powerful mucus membrane that acted almost like glue, sticking the armor tightly to its body. I could affect the armor, but I could not detach it.

For a moment, I felt mentally stuck. If I could not remove the armor and the frog was immune to the fairies' magical attacks, it seemed as though the fairy village might be doomed.

Then I noticed something that did not make sense. Geomancy should have been magic, yet I had just used it to affect the frog's supposedly anti-magic armor. Even though I had not been able to pull the armor off, my power had still moved it. I started wondering whether what I had always called Geomancy was actually magic at all.

I thought about how many abilities that are described as magical can look almost indistinguishable from psychic powers. If someone has a magical ability to create and manipulate fire, for example, that sounds very similar to pyrokinesis. The difference is that pyrokinesis would not traditionally use magic or mana; it would use psi as a psychic ability. That made me wonder whether my Geomancy was more accurately Geokinesis.

That realization made me reconsider the fairies too. I had assumed that all of their powers were magical simply because they were fairies, but perhaps some of them actually had psychic abilities instead.

I yelled for all of the magic fairies to fall back and for all of the psychic fairies to move to the front line. The magical fairies retreated, and the psychic fairies advanced toward the armored frog kaiju.

Their attacks worked. The frog was struck by pyrokinetic flames, then frozen by cryokinetic ice, and finally blasted away by electrokinetic lightning bolts. The psychic attacks were able to bypass the protection of the sky iron samurai armor, allowing us to defeat the giant humanoid frog kaiju.

The experience made me reflect on the difference between magical powers and psychic powers. I wondered whether any of what I do in my dreams is really magic at all. Dreams are part of the mind, and powers of the mind are normally described as psychic, so perhaps all of the abilities I have been calling magic in the Storyverse are technically psychic abilities instead. I was not certain that this was true, but it became an interesting theory for me to consider.
