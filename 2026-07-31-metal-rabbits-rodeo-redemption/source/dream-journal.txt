The Metal Rabbit’s Rodeo Redemption

I immediately encountered a strange dream character in what seemed to be an empty, dark void. It looked like a humanoid skeletal rabbit made of orange metal. Its body was covered in grime and dirt, its eyes glowed bright red, and two large buck teeth protruded from its mouth.

At first, I was mostly fascinated by it. It was such an unusual design for a dream character that I found it interesting rather than threatening.

As the rabbit approached me, however, it tried to intimidate me. It asked whether I was afraid of it.

I told it that I was not.

I knew I was inside a lucid dream, which meant that I was in control. The rabbit then revealed that its goal was specifically to frighten me. I told it that it was going to have a very difficult time accomplishing that.

The rabbit raised its arms, and dark clouds of purple fog began rising all around us. The environment transformed. We still seemed to be inside some kind of void, but the atmosphere became much more ominous. Before, we had simply been standing in empty darkness. Now, the surrounding clouds made it impossible to tell what might be hiding nearby—or whether anything existed beyond what I could see.

Even then, I was not particularly intimidated.

Part of the problem was that I simply did not find rabbits frightening. The fact that this one was a grimy, skeletal, orange metal humanoid with glowing red eyes did not automatically make it scary to me.

I was much more fascinated by the fact that it seemed capable of controlling the dream world. I had encountered beings in previous dreams that possessed almost as much authorship over the environment as I did, and those encounters sometimes led to interesting battles.

This situation did not initially feel like a battle.

That changed when the rabbit summoned a group of creepy clowns.

The clowns clawed their way out of the ground like zombies. They were decayed and skeletal, but I still was not afraid. I had never found clowns especially frightening either.

The rabbit seemed frustrated by my reaction.

“What about clowns with chainsaws?” it asked.

The zombie clowns reached directly into their own rib cages and pulled out chainsaws. They started the weapons and advanced toward me.

I summoned Silonis, my rainbow crystal Sword of Life.

Instead of immediately fighting the clowns, I allowed them to strike me with their chainsaws. Every injury they caused was instantly repaired by the rainbow crystal energy radiating from Silonis.

I wanted to demonstrate to the rabbit that physical attacks were useless against me. As long as I possessed the Sword of Life, I could simply heal from whatever damage its creatures inflicted.

The rabbit manipulated the world again.

A black humanoid spider with glowing red eyes appeared and swung rapidly past me. Because it possessed many arms, one of its hands managed to catch Silonis as it passed. The sword did not merely fall into the spider’s grip. It became stuck to the creature’s palm, held in place by the spider’s natural adhesive.

The metal rabbit began cackling maniacally while the chainsaw-wielding zombie clowns continued closing in around me.

I remained calm.

I explained to the rabbit that stealing Silonis did not matter. The Sword of Life had a mind of its own. It was alive in its own way and could move or fly without my assistance.

Silonis attempted to pull itself free, but the spider’s palm remained firmly attached to it.

Since the spider could not release the sword, Silonis twisted violently through the air and sliced off the creature’s hand. The severed hand remained stuck to the blade as Silonis flew back into my grasp.

To prevent anything else from stealing the sword, I used Silonis to create a dome-shaped rainbow crystal force field around myself. I told the rabbit that it could no longer reach me. The barrier was essentially indestructible.

The rabbit found another weakness.

The force field protected me from above and around the sides, but it did not extend beneath my feet.

A small mole man burst out of the ground inside the barrier.

The creature was wild and crazed, but it did not seem especially strong or destructive. It was only about the size of a puppy or a large rat.

Then another mole man appeared.

And another.

Within moments, there were nearly a hundred of them erupting from the ground. They swarmed over my body, crawling across one another and piling on top of me.

This was the first attack that genuinely surprised me.

The mole men were not particularly dangerous individually, but their overwhelming numbers created the sensation that I might be crushed beneath their combined weight.

Even then, I still felt that I was in control.

I dismissed the rainbow crystal force field and activated my lucid light. I launched myself upward into the air, carrying the mole men with me. Once I was high enough, I released a powerful shock wave of lucid light.

The blast threw the mole men away from my body and knocked back all the creatures the rabbit had summoned.

The rabbit itself remained standing.

Its red eyes glowed even brighter as it cackled once again.

“Nothing you do will allow you to escape me,” it declared.

The rabbit began to grow.

Its orange metal body expanded until it became a massive giant towering over me.

I looked up at it.

“I don’t need to escape,” I replied.

I fired my lucid light into the distance and used it to create a gigantic metal carrot.

The rabbit’s entire disposition changed immediately.

Its threatening demeanor vanished. It stared at the carrot in astonishment, rushed over to it, and asked, almost gratefully, “Is this for me?”

I told it that it was.

The rabbit seemed genuinely confused.

“Why?” it asked. “All I’ve tried to do this entire time is scare you.”

I told it that this was true, but it had not done a very good job.

Then I experienced a strange intuition.

I told the rabbit that it should stop trying to frighten people and become a rodeo cowboy instead.

The rabbit looked at me with sudden hope.

“You really think I have what it takes?” it asked.

I told it that I did.

I could not explain exactly why, but beneath its monstrous appearance, the rabbit seemed to possess a strange southern charm. Despite being a grimy orange metal skeletal creature with glowing red eyes, I felt that becoming a rodeo cowboy would suit it far better than trying to terrify people.

When it was time for us to part ways, both of us naturally began speaking with slight southern accents.

We exchanged a cheerful “Howdy” and said goodbye.

The rabbit allowed me to fly away.

As I traveled farther from it, the dark void gradually became less oppressive. I emerged into Story-Verse space and continued flying toward the distant bubble universes.

Then the dream ended.
