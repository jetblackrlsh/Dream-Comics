Dream Journal Reconstruction

I began the dream flying over a city, which is something I have done in many of my dreams. Usually, when I fly over a city, I eventually descend toward the streets to patrol the area, search for villains, or respond to some kind of threat.

This time, I did the opposite.

Instead of flying down toward the city, I flew higher.

I continued ascending until I disappeared into the clouds. As I passed through them, I discovered a place that seemed to exist between realms.

There was a floating tavern there called the Cloud Tavern.

The Cloud Tavern was a gathering place for heroes from many different kinds of worlds. Traditional superheroes mingled with fantasy adventurers. Some heroes wore colorful costumes, masks, and capes and appeared to maintain secret identities. Others wore robes or armor and belonged to fantasy-style hero guilds. There were elves, magical warriors, and even benevolent demons among the patrons.

The mixture created some unusual scenes.

I saw a strong dwarf arm wrestling with an enormous, muscular superhero who looked like the kind of powerhouse who could trade punches with the Hulk.

Elsewhere, a magic-based superhero carefully examined the terms of a pact alongside one of the demonic heroes from a guild.

Throughout the tavern, heroes socialized, drank, ate, and celebrated their accomplishments.

Large screens mounted around the Cloud Tavern displayed alerts whenever trouble occurred somewhere in one of the realms.

There were three particularly important doors.

The center door was the one I had entered through. It served as the ordinary entrance to the Cloud Tavern. Someone could reach it by flying upward through the clouds and entering this strange realm between realms.

The door on the far left was different. It was effectively an arrival door for heroes returning from missions. It could only be entered by someone who had just completed a mission somewhere else.

Heroes returning through this door approached a counter where their weapons, gadgets, and other equipment were scanned.

Afterward, they received currency called Marks.

Marks were flat pieces of gold shaped like crowns. They could be spent throughout the Cloud Tavern. Heroes could use Marks to purchase food and drinks, rent rooms, obtain weapons, and acquire other equipment and services.

The door on the far right worked in the opposite direction.

It was the departure door for heroes going on missions.

Whenever one of the screens displayed an alert that a hero believed they could handle—or an emergency appeared in their home realm—they could race through the right-hand door. The door would then take them directly to the location where the emergency was occurring.

I noticed something unusual about the equipment carried by the heroes.

It did not matter whether someone carried a medieval sword or used futuristic superhero technology. All of the equipment registered with the Cloud Tavern had a distinctive grid pattern running across it. The grid resembled glowing neon-green circuitry.

When I approached the bar, I asked why everyone's equipment had this pattern.

Someone working there explained that the Cloud Tavern used its equipment to track heroic accomplishments.

The gear could record things such as how many successful strikes a hero landed against villains, how many evil creatures they defeated or slew, and how many villains they captured.

Not every hero killed their enemies. Many superheroes captured villains and delivered them to places such as supermax prisons designed to contain supervillains. However, some of the fantasy heroes used lethal weapons, and certain antiheroes were willing to use things like guns.

The system accounted for all of these different approaches.

The accomplishments recorded by the equipment determined how many Marks a hero earned when returning from a mission.

At some point, the person behind the bar noticed Silonus.

Silonus is my Rainbow Crystal Sword, also known as the Life Sword. It is one of the most powerful artifacts in the Storyverse.

The bartender reached toward Silonus and tried to take it from me.

They told me that I would not need my sword because it wasn't registered as one of the Cloud Tavern's official pieces of gear. Since it could not track my accomplishments, I would not be able to earn Marks while using it.

They offered to take Silonus off my hands and replace it with a "real" sword.

In their other hand, they showed me a sword covered in the glowing neon-green circuit grid.

I immediately rejected the offer.

Using Silonus, I generated a rainbow crystal force field that repelled the bartender's hand and prevented them from taking the sword.

I explained that there was far more to the Rainbow Crystal Sword than most people realized.

I suspected that they knew Silonus was not some worthless blade and that this might have been part of the reason they were so eager to take it from me.

But there was another important detail they had overlooked.

Silonus wasn't merely a magical sword made from colorful crystals.

It also contained futuristic technology.

I held Silonus near the sword the bartender had offered me.

There was a small red gem embedded in Silonus's hilt. I used it to scan the Cloud Tavern sword and analyze its technology.

Silonus adapted.

The same glowing circuit grid appeared across the Rainbow Crystal Sword.

The neon-green circuitry blended with the sword's colorful crystal structure, creating a fusion of magical crystal and futuristic technology.

I then approached the place where returning heroes had their equipment scanned to calculate their Marks.

The people there were confused.

Even if Silonus had somehow copied their technology, I had only upgraded the sword moments earlier. According to their understanding of the system, there shouldn't have been any heroic accomplishments recorded on it yet.

I explained that they still didn't understand what Silonus could do.

The sword hadn't simply copied the ability to begin counting my accomplishments from that moment onward.

It had adapted the technology so that my previous accomplishments could also be recognized.

In other words, Silonus had generated a backlog.

They were skeptical, but they agreed to scan the Rainbow Crystal Sword.

The result shocked everyone.

The system effectively registered an infinite number of Marks.

They couldn't understand how that was possible.

There was no way I could have personally slain an infinite number of evil creatures or captured an infinite number of villains.

I explained that they were measuring my accomplishments incorrectly.

One of my most important responsibilities in the Storyverse is protecting the Rainbow Crystal Sword itself.

Silonus is such a powerful artifact that keeping it out of the hands of evil is an act of protecting the Storyverse.

Whenever I prevent someone evil from taking Silonus, I am not merely protecting one person, one city, or even one realm.

I am potentially protecting the entire Storyverse.

And I haven't had to defend Silonus only once or twice.

It feels as though I am almost constantly protecting the Rainbow Crystal Sword from people and forces that want to take it from me.

When the Cloud Tavern's system translated that history into its own method of measuring heroic accomplishments, the resulting number of Marks was effectively infinite.

My reputation inside the tavern changed almost instantly.

I had entered the Cloud Tavern as an unknown newcomer who didn't even understand how its currency or equipment worked.

Now the other heroes realized that I was anything but inexperienced.

I had gotten around.

I knew what I was doing.

And Silonus had the record to prove it.
