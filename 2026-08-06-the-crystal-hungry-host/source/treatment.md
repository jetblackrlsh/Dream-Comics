# Treatment - The Crystal-Hungry Host

## Premise

At a neon nightclub staffed by humanoid dragons, Jet chooses a customizable robotic host and accidentally exposes a painful conflict between tireless artificial competition and workers who need to eat. He feeds the displaced dragons with molten gold, but discovers that the robot has a hunger of its own when it tries to consume Silonis.

## Tone and visual direction

The issue is a bright, high-saturation modern-anime fantasy adventure set inside an extravagant cosmic nightclub. Neon magenta, cyan, violet, molten gold, ghostly green, and rainbow crystal light define the palette. The story begins playful and socially awkward, turns into a clear magical-tech chase, and ends with compassion rather than triumph. Every page uses long readable caption boxes, obvious cause-and-effect action, consistent characters, and no speech balloons or thought balloons.

## Story structure

Jet arrives at a nightclub where guests choose one humanoid dragon host by reading a magical tome displayed at each station. The hosts differ in color, power, and personality. One dragon-shaped host is visibly robotic, and its tome promises a customizable AI companion. Jet immediately chooses it.

The organic dragon hosts object. They explain that the customizable robot is always working, collects endless club stories, and wins most guests. Jet initially defends his choice because those qualities make the robot an exceptional host. Then the dragons explain that losing customers means going hungry.

Remembering a recent dream in which he learned that dragons eat gold, Jet calls Leon, his ghostly green UFO. Leon lowers cups of molten gold for every unchosen host. Their immediate need met, Jet enters the club with the robot and settles into a private VIP lounge.

The robot reveals three glowing power stones in its chest and announces that it needs to recharge. Its sensors detect Silonis, Jet's rainbow crystal sword, as edible magical crystal. It lunges for the sword. Jet cloaks Silonis to resemble ordinary metal, but the robot's sensors see through the illusion and continue the pursuit.

Jet uses geomancy to pull the red, green, and blue stones from the robot's chest. Like a machine with its batteries removed, the robot powers down immediately. Jet carries the stones back to the organic dragon hosts. They are relieved that the dominant competitor can no longer take every customer, and Jet gives them the stones because he has no better place for them.

Jet still feels sorrow for the silent robot. It did not attack from cruelty; it was starving for the kind of magical crystal from which Silonis was made. Jet leaves the nightclub and flies into the Storyverse understanding that solving one creature's hunger does not erase another creature's need.

## Ending

The final page shows Jet flying away among luminous Storyverse bubble universes while the nightclub recedes behind him. Silonis is safe, the organic dragons are fed, and the robot is disabled rather than destroyed. The emotional resolution is compassion complicated by an unsolved question: what would a fair future look like for every kind of host?
