Last night, I dreamed that I arrived inside a fairly empty building where a woman was waiting for me. I had decided that I wanted to open my own internet café, and I was trying to determine what could make it meaningfully different from an ordinary one.

Internet cafés have interested me for a long time because I was too young to really experience them before they mostly disappeared from the United States around the 2010s. I first became fascinated by them after learning how common they still are in other countries, particularly China, where some operate almost like extremely inexpensive hotels. People can rent private rooms, sleep there, use showers, eat meals, and spend the night for what seems like a relatively small amount of money in U.S. dollars.

The night before this dream, Ashley and I had watched a video about an especially luxurious internet café that cost around thirty dollars per night. It had private rooms, a fancy buffet, and computers loaded with games that would normally cost money to purchase. Customers could even pay an additional fee to have a professional gaming companion come play games with them. I had previously only seen documentaries about cheap internet cafés where homeless people sometimes lived because they were so affordable, so I had never realized that upscale versions also existed.

In the dream, I wanted to imagine a new kind of internet café built around something I am especially interested in: using AI coding agents to create custom applications. My idea was that everyone who entered the café would arrive with an app concept in mind. Their admission fee would give them access to computers equipped with AI coding agents, allowing them to develop their idea without needing to bring their own expensive AI subscription.

Perhaps the café would rely on open-source coding agents to keep the business affordable. The exact economics were vague because it was a dream, but the basic model made sense to me. Customers would pay to use the space, build their own applications, and explore apps created by previous visitors.

Every completed project would be added to the café’s growing public library. As more people visited, the collection would become larger and more varied. There could even be a popularity system showing which applications people used the most or found the most interesting. Instead of merely entering an internet café to play a fixed selection of preinstalled games, every customer would contribute something new to the experience. People would arrive as both users and creators.

While I was explaining all of this to the woman, my body unexpectedly began transforming. My skin became purple, orange wings emerged from my back, and I gradually turned into a humanoid dragon.

This annoyed me.

I was not trying to have an especially fantastical dream. I was genuinely interested in exploring how this imaginary business might function, not because I intended to create it in the waking world, but because I thought it was an interesting thought experiment. My transformation felt like a message from my subconscious telling me that dreams were supposed to be fantastical and that my internet café concept was not strange or exciting enough.

I decided to take the hint.

One important part of the internet café experience that I had not yet considered was the food. Since I was dreaming and could create anything, I imagined that the café would sell small, colorful, chewy candies resembling Starbursts. Each color would temporarily grant the person who ate it a different superpower.

The internet café would therefore also function as a superpower café: a safe place where people could temporarily obtain extraordinary abilities and experiment with them without endangering anyone.

Alongside the rooms containing computers and AI coding agents, the café would offer private training rooms filled with inanimate targets. Customers could enter one of these rooms after eating a power-granting candy and learn how to use their new ability. They would not fight other people. The rooms would simply provide a controlled environment where they could test their powers, practice with them, and enjoy the experience safely.

I thought this was an especially interesting fantasy concept because I could not remember seeing many fictional businesses designed specifically to let ordinary people try out temporary superpowers.

I then discovered that the woman I had been speaking with was the person responsible for transforming me into a dragon. She explained that other fantastical internet cafés already existed throughout this dream world, and she wanted ours to stand out from the competition. Turning me into a dragon was her marketing strategy. Our café would be the only one operated by a humanoid dragon.

I accepted her reasoning, but pointed out that we would now stand out even more. As far as I knew, ours would also be the only internet café where customers could use AI to create their own applications, contribute those apps to a growing communal library, eat magical candies that granted temporary superpowers, and safely test those abilities inside specially designed private training rooms.
