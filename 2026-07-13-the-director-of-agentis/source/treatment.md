# Treatment

## Logline

After inducing lucidity through sleep paralysis, Jet is named director of a futuristic organization staffed by humanlike AI agents and must decide what the overwhelming command center is actually for.

## Central conflict

Agentis gives physical form to Jet's real habit of using AI agents to make things. Seeing every available agent waiting at once turns a familiar creative tool into an intimidating management problem: Jet has more potential help than he has clearly defined work, and he does not yet know whether that should become a company, a larger media site, or anything new at all.

## Theme

A change in perspective can make familiar circumstances feel strange, reveal hidden capacity, and suggest a direction without demanding an immediate final answer.

## Emotional arc

Jet begins alert but disoriented, amused by the possibility that he leads a spy organization. Recognition replaces confusion when he realizes Agentis is a cyberspace reflection of his waking workflow. That recognition becomes pressure as the silent crowd exposes his discomfort with parallel coordination and his uncertainty about entrepreneurship. Jet regains agency by rejecting the demand for an instant grand company and giving the agents one honest, bounded assignment: map the work he already does and connect it into a possible media landscape. He ends thoughtful, calmer, and open to possibilities he does not need to settle today.

## Ending

The chaotic command center reorganizes into a luminous map that connects Jet's comics, website, support options, and other media without pretending they are already a finished company. Standing before the same agents, Jet sees not an unused workforce or a failed business, but a field of possible collaborators. The circumstances have not changed; his way of seeing them has.
