The Dinosaur Chef
I arrived in what appeared to be a prehistoric world, surrounded by the dense vegetation of an ancient jungle.
Standing directly in front of me was a small dinosaur. It did not seem hostile or aggressive. In fact, it looked rather dopey, staring blankly at me without making any attempt to attack. Its body had a green-and-orange color scheme that reminded me of a mango.
As I observed the creature, a woman approached through the jungle.
She wore a dress woven from wicker and a wide sun hat. Clusters of bright red berries dangled from the hat’s brim on every side. The hat cast such a deep shadow over her head that I could not see her face at all. Her entire face appeared shrouded in darkness, with only her glowing eyes visible. Her pupils burned red from within the shadow.
She stopped near me and casually asked, “Would you like some bacon?”
I hesitated.
“I don’t really eat bacon that often,” I told her.
“You don’t eat bacon ever?” she asked.
“It’s not that I’ve never eaten bacon,” I explained. “I just don’t eat it most of the time.”
She leaned toward me with sudden enthusiasm.
“Yeah, but have you ever had dinosaur bacon?”
“Dinosaur bacon?” I repeated. “I’m not sure I’m necessarily interested in that.”
“Come on,” she said. “Aren’t you curious? Kill this dinosaur, and I’ll make you some dinosaur bacon.”
I glanced down at the little green-and-orange dinosaur, which was still standing harmlessly in front of us.
“Are you some kind of dinosaur chef?” I asked.
“Yes,” she answered.
She motioned to one side of the jungle. Despite the prehistoric surroundings, an entire outdoor kitchen had been constructed there, complete with counters, utensils, and cooking equipment.
“I love cooking dinosaurs,” she told me. “Dinosaur meat is incredible.”
I looked back at the clusters of red berries hanging from her hat.
“What are those berries?” I asked.
“These?” She gestured toward them. “These are what make dinosaurs taste good.”
“Taste good?” I replied. “I thought you said dinosaurs were already delicious.”
“They are,” she insisted, “but only if you mix in these berries. Otherwise, dinosaur meat has this spoiled poultry taste.”
That made her entire proposal seem even less appealing.
“If dinosaurs don’t actually taste good unless you use the berries,” I said, “then maybe you shouldn’t eat the dinosaur at all. You should just eat the berries.”
“No, no, no, no, no,” she said. “You don’t understand. You have to eat the dinosaurs. Once you eat one, you’ll understand why you should eat them.”
I firmly refused.
“I’m not going to kill the dinosaur,” I told her. “It isn’t even aggressive. And from what you’ve said, it doesn’t sound like the meat tastes good anyway. Killing it for food I probably wouldn’t enjoy would just be a waste.”
Her demeanor immediately changed.
The woman became hostile and reached into the outdoor kitchen, pulling out a large knife. She extended it toward me.
“Take this knife,” she demanded. “Kill the dinosaur, or we’re going to have some problems.”
“No,” I said. “I’m not killing it.”
Without warning, she stabbed me.
“I told you we were going to have some problems,” she said.
Fortunately, I had my life-sword, Silonis, with me. Silonis was a futuristic sword made from rainbow crystal, capable of channeling restorative energy. I activated its power and healed the stab wound.
I explained that threats of violence were not going to force me to obey her. No matter what injuries she inflicted, I could use Silonis to regenerate and heal myself.
She pulled her knife free and stared at me through the darkness beneath her hat.
“You think that’s going to be enough to stop me?” she asked. “Eating dinosaurs makes you big and strong.”
Her body suddenly began to grow.
She expanded into a giant towering above the jungle and raised one enormous foot over me, preparing to crush me beneath it.
I lifted Silonis and generated a rainbow crystal energy barrier just as her foot came crashing down. The barrier caught the stomp, holding her immense weight above me.
As I looked upward through the glowing crystal shield, her foot began to change.
The transformation spread through the rest of her body. For the first time, the shadow over her face shifted enough for me to see what she was becoming.
She transformed into a gigantic humanoid dinosaur woman.
She still wore the same wicker dress and the same sun hat covered in dangling red berries, but her body was now entirely dinosaur-like. She towered above me as an enormous reptilian humanoid, her glowing red eyes staring down from beneath the brim of her hat.
“You are what you eat,” she declared.
I realized that her power operated according to a strange but fitting piece of dream logic. By eating dinosaurs, she had gained the ability to become one.
I dismissed the rainbow crystal barrier and propelled myself backward with Lucid Light, performing a rapid back dash before her giant foot could come crashing fully to the ground.
Once I had created enough distance between us, I summoned Tetheron, my golden energy tether.
I cast the glowing lasso around both the dinosaur woman and the small dopey dinosaur, magically bonding their minds together.
The little dinosaur’s thoughts began flowing directly into her consciousness.
Through its perspective, she witnessed memories of all the times she had prepared and cooked dinosaur meat in front of other living dinosaurs. She experienced their fear, grief, and horror as though those emotions were her own.
Now that she had transformed into a dinosaur herself, the weight of those memories affected her differently. She was no longer viewing the creatures merely as ingredients. She was seeing her actions through the mind of one of the animals she had hunted and eaten.
The experience overwhelmed her.
“Oh no,” she cried. “What have I done?”
She became visibly sick and traumatized by everything she was seeing.
I tried to calm her.
“If you feel bad about what you did, you can stop,” I told her. “You could eat the berries instead. Maybe you could mix them with dough or cook them into something else. If what you like is the texture of the meat combined with the berries, you could create a different kind of food that gives you a similar experience without killing dinosaurs.”
“Meat is everything,” she snarled.
Despite the thoughts and emotions she had just experienced, her carnivorous instincts still controlled her.
Then she looked directly at me.
“I know what I can eat,” she said. “I can eat you.”
She reached down and attempted to grab me.
I immediately surrounded my body with Lucid Light, forming a complete suit of glowing power armor. The armor’s propulsion systems ignited, and I blasted upward before her hands could close around me.
The dinosaur woman chased after me, stretching and reaching as high as she could. However, her transformation had limits. She had already reached the maximum size of her dinosaur form and could not continue growing indefinitely.
The confrontation became a simple contest of height.
I flew higher and higher until I was far beyond her reach. She remained below, towering over the prehistoric jungle but unable to follow me into the sky.
Once I knew she could no longer catch me, I accelerated away from the prehistoric world and flew into the Storyverse.
