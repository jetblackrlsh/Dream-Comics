Dream Journal Reconstruction
I began the dream flying through a city like a superhero, as I have in many of my dreams. While passing over the streets, I noticed a gigantic, blob-like green slime monster below me. It was not actually doing anything dangerous, so I simply continued flying past it.
The slime monster called out to me, asking whether I was going to try to stop it. It then formed a massive slime tentacle and slammed it into the road, seemingly attempting to provoke a heroic response from me. I told it that the whole display seemed performative. I cared about helping people, but it was not actually hurting anyone. It appeared to be causing a spectacle merely to attract my attention.
In response, the creature lifted its tentacle and revealed that a woman was trapped inside the slime. It challenged me by asking, “Does this look performative?”
I realized that someone genuinely was in danger after all. I drew Silonis, the Rainbow Crystal Sword of Life and one of the most powerful artifacts in the Storyverse. Using its control over life energy, I fused the life energy of the woman with that of the slime monster. Their bodies merged into a gigantic green slime woman, immediately changing the situation from one in which the woman was helplessly trapped into one in which I had time and control to act.
Both sides of the fusion were horrified by what I had done. The woman did not want to exist as a strange slime creature and felt that she would almost rather have remained in mortal danger than become one. The slime monster was equally disturbed by being forced into a humanoid form and would have preferred that I destroy it rather than leave it trapped that way.
I assured them that the fusion was only a temporary measure. I used Silonis again, slicing through the life-energy connection I had created and carefully separating the two beings. The woman returned to her own body, and the slime monster became an independent entity again. I explained that fusing them had simply been a quick way to transform an immediate hostage situation into one where I could safely do something about it.
I then cast the Porta spell, using its power to open a portal, and banished the slime monster through it. The rescued woman was confused and asked whether I had sent the creature into some kind of prison dimension.
I told her that I had recently discovered a prison dimension in one of my previous dreams called the Glimmer. The Glimmer was primarily intended for magical enemies and resembled a spirit realm where those confined within it could no longer cause harm. However, I had chosen not to send the slime monster there. Instead, I had sent it into an Empty Dimension.
She asked what that meant. She wondered whether the Empty Dimension was a place without time or space. I admitted that I did not know exactly how to define it. The best comparison I could offer was that it was like being alone inside one’s own head—a place where the slime monster could be entirely by itself and think about its actions.
As I considered my own explanation, the idea began to sound appealing. Being alone with one’s thoughts did not seem inherently unpleasant to me. I genuinely enjoyed having the freedom to narrate my life aloud, reflect, and exist privately with my own thoughts. I thought, “Fuck it,” opened another portal, and sent myself into a world where I could experience that kind of solitude for myself.
Upon entering the other world, I split into two versions of myself. One version was an observer who simply watched what happened, while the other version actively lived inside the dimension.
The version of me living there was not completely alone. I encountered people I recognized from the waking world, but there was something distinctly different about how I interacted with them. The observer version of me remained apart and watched, while the resident version seemed free to do more or less whatever he wanted.
I still followed the unconventional habits and routines that felt natural to me, such as eating only once a day and sleeping on the floor. However, nobody treated those choices as strange. No one made a fuss about the things I enjoyed doing or the way I preferred to live. They simply allowed me to be myself.
The place felt almost like a digital dream world, yet there was no major crisis or obvious hidden danger. For the most part, everything was simply fine. I possessed a profound sense of freedom, but it was different from the freedom I normally experienced in a lucid dream. It was not the spectacular freedom of having superpowers, saving people, or fighting villains. It was the quiet freedom to live an ordinary life as myself without being judged or continually asked to justify my choices.
I remained in this world for what felt like a significant amount of time. Eventually, the observer version of me retained enough awareness of the waking world to realize that it was probably approaching eight in the morning. I thought that I should be getting up.
At that moment, the resident version of me began speaking directly to the observer version. His voice and reasoning felt almost like the temptation of a siren. He told me that this was a perfect world where we did not have to worry about anything. We could be ourselves, do whatever felt natural, and remain in this dimension forever. There was no need for me to return to the waking world at all.
Despite how pleasant and accepting the dimension felt, I refused. I told him that I was good and that I had other things I needed to do. Then I allowed myself to wake up.
