Dream Journal Reconstruction
My dream began as many of my dreams do, with me flying through a city. As I moved through the sky, someone standing on a rooftop caught my attention. I landed near them and saw someone who appeared fairly normal, with brown hair and possibly green eyes. I could not remember their gender with certainty. I believed they initially appeared to be a man, but later in the encounter they seemed to become a woman.

Even though they were not visibly demonstrating any unusual abilities, I somehow recognized that they possessed a special power. It felt like one of the “dream downloads” I sometimes experience, when information suddenly enters my mind even though I should have no way of knowing it. I confronted the person without hostility and asked what their power was. They were caught off guard because they had a secret identity to protect, and people normally could not recognize them while they were in their ordinary form. I asked whether they were a superhero, and they confirmed that they were.

They activated their power by growing hairs from their body and creating balloons at the ends of those hairs. At first, I thought it was a rather strange ability, but they assured me that it was more useful than I imagined. They covered their body in hair without producing balloons, giving themselves the appearance of Bigfoot or a Sasquatch. I believed this might have been when their appearance changed from male to female. It was not merely the result of having longer hair; their voice and body proportions also changed. I wondered whether changing gender was another part of their powers and an especially effective way of protecting their identity, although I could not be certain whether that was truly an ability or simply dream logic.

The hero began demonstrating how versatile the hair could be. They generated a mass of hair from one hand, extending it into a long whip that could be swung as a weapon. They could also throw the hair toward a flagpole, wrap it around the pole, and use it to swing between buildings. For another method of travel, they grew a hair from their back and formed a large balloon at its tip. The balloon lifted them into the sky, but because floating was not the same as controlled flight, they used the hair whip to latch onto objects and pull themselves in different directions.

They could also detach their hairs and root them into other things. They placed one of the hairs onto a building, commanded it to root itself into the structure, and then created an enormous balloon at its upper tip. The balloon began lifting the entire building out of the ground. I was alarmed and warned the hero that they were going too far. I thought the building’s plumbing, electricity, and other infrastructure would be torn apart, and I told them that this seemed more like something a supervillain would do.

The hero then explained that their rooting and unrooting powers were not limited to their hairs. The balloon could lift the building because they had also unrooted the structure itself. Whenever they unrooted one of their hairs, the process was painless and did not make them bleed. Similarly, although temporarily uprooting a structure could appear to damage pipes or other connections, rerooting it was a seamless process that restored everything completely. They demonstrated this by rooting the building back into the ground without leaving it damaged.

They then created an even larger balloon and unrooted the entire city, lifting it into the sky. Afterward, they rerooted the city in the ground and restored it. This incredible display attracted the attention of the authorities, who demanded to know whether the hero had truly lifted the whole city. I vouched for them because, despite the frightening scale of their abilities, I genuinely believed their heart was in the right place. Their powers seemed much scarier on the surface than they actually were when used responsibly. However, the authorities still wanted proof that this person used those gifts to help people rather than merely demonstrating what they could do.

We transitioned to a hospital, where the hero showed us how they normally used their abilities. Covered in hair like a friendly Sasquatch, they visited sick children and created balloons for them. The children were delighted that someone who looked like Bigfoot had come to entertain them.

The most extraordinary application of the hero’s power involved organ transplants. If a patient needed a new heart and a viable donor organ was available, the hero could unroot the patient’s failing heart and root the healthy heart into its place. Ordinarily, removing someone’s heart in that way would kill them, but the special nature of the rooting process restored the patient as the new organ was attached. It avoided the dangerous and painstaking inconsistencies of conventional surgery because the unrooting and rerooting happened perfectly.

I was amazed that someone capable of lifting an entire city had not chosen to spend their life fighting supervillains or stopping bank robberies. They could have been an enormous asset against massive threats, and if they had been evil, their unrooting power could have caused untold destruction. Instead, they had found a compassionate niche that brought all of their unusual abilities together: entertaining children, helping people in hospitals, and performing vital surgeries. Their power initially seemed bizarre and frightening, but they understood exactly how to use it to do something wonderful. That was essentially where the dream ended.
