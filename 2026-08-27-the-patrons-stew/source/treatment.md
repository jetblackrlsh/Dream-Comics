# Treatment - The Patron's Stew

## Logline

After choosing Apollo as his warlock patron, Jet survives a flooded mineshaft and defeats a giant bat, only to discover that his divine quest was a grocery errand—and that healing the wounded creature could condemn it to being harvested forever.

## Protagonist and goal

Jet is the protagonist. He wants to become a capable warlock by forming a patron-familiar-focus bond and proving that he can carry out Apollo's first quest responsibly.

## Blocker

Apollo sends Jet into a lightless, flooded mineshaft where the sun god's power cannot easily reach him. Contaminated still water threatens Jet and Second Brain, and a gigantic bat attempts to steal the Rainbow Crystal Sword of Life.

## Stakes

Jet and Second Brain could become sick, drown, lose the Storyverse's most powerful artifact, or die fighting the bat. After the battle, the moral stakes become equally important: Jet's healing power could be used to perpetuate the bat's suffering.

## Causal story chain

1. Jet wants to become a warlock, so he chooses Apollo, Second Brain, and the Rainbow Crystal Sword of Life to fill the role's three requirements.
2. Apollo accepts Jet as a warlock and therefore sends him on a quest into a place outside Apollo's bright domain.
3. The dark mine hides deep contaminated water, so Jet uses the Sword's explosive barrier and healing power to protect himself and Second Brain.
4. The Sword's power attracts a giant bat, which carries Jet out of the mine when he refuses to let go.
5. Returning to the swamp lets Apollo reach the fight with sunlight, so the bat is stunned.
6. Second Brain merges with the Sword, giving Jet enough lucid lightning to sever the bat's wings without killing it.
7. Apollo reveals that the wings were stew ingredients, transforming a heroic victory into an absurd and ethically troubling commission.
8. Jet considers healing the bat, but Second Brain explains that instant healing could allow Apollo to harvest it repeatedly.
9. Jet leaves the bat to recover naturally, choosing restraint over a superficially merciful act that would enable endless exploitation.

## Central conflict

Jet initially sees patron service as a straightforward exchange of quests for power. The physical conflict tests whether he can survive conditions designed around his patron's limitations. The final moral conflict tests whether obedience and magical healing are automatically good when a patron can exploit both.

## Theme

Compassion requires attention to consequences. A healing action is not truly merciful when it sustains a system that repeatedly causes the same harm.

## Emotional arc

Jet begins excited and methodical, confidently assembling the perfect warlock identity. He treats Apollo's dangerous assignment as reasonable work beyond the god's domain and responds to every threat with decisive competence. The stew revelation changes the mood from heroic triumph to cosmic absurdity. Jet's final hesitation shows that his conscience, not his patron, determines how he will use the Sword of Life.

## Ending and final change

The final page contrasts Apollo departing with the severed wings against the injured bat sheltered in the mineshaft. Jet lowers the healing Sword after Second Brain warns him about endless wing farming. He remains Apollo's warlock, but he is no longer uncritically obedient: he has learned to judge each use of power by the future it creates.
