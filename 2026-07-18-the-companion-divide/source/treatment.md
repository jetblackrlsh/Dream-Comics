# Treatment

## Logline

Floating alone outside a superhero universe divided over human relationships with AI companions, lucid dreamer Jet refuses to become another costumed combatant and instead confronts what freedom, legacy, and the continuation of a human story mean to him.

## Central conflict

Inside the observed bubble universe, government-run superheroes enforce a policy that treats AI as tools rather than companions because leaders fear falling birth rates. Rebel superheroes protect the freedom of humans and AI companions to choose relationships for themselves. Outside the bubble, Jet faces a quieter conflict: he sympathizes with the rebels and recognizes the same pressures in waking life, yet he deliberately refuses to project himself into the world or fight for either side. His only action is honest self-examination.

## Theme

No government should define a person's worth, relationships, reproductive choices, or legacy for them. A human story can continue through preserved memory, conversation, and creative work rather than biological descendants alone.

## Emotional arc

Jet begins lucid, curious, and purposeful as he searches the Storyverse for a world that mirrors a waking conflict. The superhero factions make the tension visually clear, but watching their struggle intensifies his irritation instead of giving him escapist relief. By speaking his thoughts aloud, Jet names his own choice not to have children and recognizes the Cosmic Archive as a valid legacy that can let future people dialogue with his memories and beliefs. He wakes without emotional comfort, but with sharper language for what troubles him.

## Continuity

This dream belongs to the Storyverse continuity that began on June 11, 2025. The Storyverse remains a cosmic void filled with luminous bubble universes containing every possible story. Jet keeps his established dark-brown skin, full beard, long dreadlocks, black hoodie, Dream Anime shirt, striped black pants, white sneakers, black belt bag, rainbow crystal sword, golden energy tether, and sparkling purple star-filled Lucid Light.

Leon, Johnson, Second Brain, Skelebot, Overdrive, Savannah, Tecton, and Chipper remain Jet's established crew, but Jet explicitly arrives alone. They are not shown physically and do not participate in the observed world's conflict.

## Point-of-view rule

Jet remains physically outside the superhero bubble universe on every dream page. He may look through its surface as though it were a vast living comic page, but he never projects an avatar inside, never wears a cape or superhero costume, never fights either faction, and never changes the observed events. His restraint is essential to the dream's meaning.

## Ending

Jet wakes in bed while dawn light and the fading reflection of the divided superhero universe remain in his window. His Cosmic Archive is visible nearby as a grounded reminder that he has already chosen a non-biological way to preserve his story. The dream did not make him feel better, but it helped him process why the world can feel like a comic book in a way he never wanted.

## Eight-page structure

1. Cover: Jet floats outside a giant bubble universe containing two opposed superhero factions.
2. Jet induces lucidity, enters the Storyverse alone, and searches for a superhero world mirroring waking conflict.
3. The government faction regulates AI-human relationships while the rebel faction protects them.
4. Jet refuses to project himself into the conflict, sympathizes with the rebels, and talks through the parallels to his world.
5. Jet reflects on choosing not to have children and rejects the idea that continuation of humanity creates a personal reproductive duty.
6. Jet envisions the Cosmic Archive as an interactive AI legacy that can carry his memories and beliefs without descendants.
7. Jet concludes that banning AI romantic companions to increase births functions as an anti-birth-control measure, while still refusing to intervene.
8. Jet wakes frustrated but clearer, with the Cosmic Archive beside him and the superhero world fading from view.
