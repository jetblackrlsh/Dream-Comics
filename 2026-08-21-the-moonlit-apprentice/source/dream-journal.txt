Dream Journal Reconstruction

I began the dream as a humanoid black sheep-dragon. It was a strange combination that initially felt very disorienting. The basic shape of my body resembled a humanoid sheep more than a dragon: I was covered in black wool and had the overall features of a sheep standing and moving like a person. However, I also had enormous batlike dragon wings and the ability to breathe fire.

Were-creatures were fairly normal within the world of the dream. I understood “were-creature” as a broad term encompassing almost anything nonhuman that possessed human-level intelligence. There seemed to be a spectrum. Some were-creatures might simply resemble ordinary animals or even objects while thinking and speaking like humans, while others were humanoid versions of animals or objects. What made my situation so unusual was not merely that I had encountered a were-creature. I was one myself, and I was apparently a hybrid of multiple types of were-creatures.

I took flight and began flapping my enormous dragon wings through the sky. Beneath me stretched a wooded landscape that looked as though it belonged to a medieval fantasy world.

As I flew, a humanoid wolf wearing metal armor rode into the area below me. He appeared to be a knight and carried a long jousting lance. He hurled the lance into the sky directly toward me, but I did not try to dodge it. When the weapon struck me, it passed straight through my body without harming me. Although my sheep-like body appeared thick and woolly, my wool behaved more like a cloud than solid fur. The lance traveled through it as though it had entered a bank of mist.

The wolf knight stared at me in complete confusion and demanded to know what had happened. I explained that my body operated much more like a cloud than he probably expected.

He protested that this made no sense and asked whether I was some kind of elemental.

“Kind of,” I told him. “I can breathe fire.”

I demonstrated by blasting fire toward him. The wolf knight screamed in alarm as the flames engulfed his metal armor. He began panicking because the heated suit made him feel as though he were being cooked alive. He shouted that he could not understand why knights wore metal armor when they went to fight dragons. The armor was becoming scalding hot around him, so he hurriedly removed it before it could burn him any worse.

I admitted that I did not really know why knights wore armor to fight dragons either. On one hand, I could understand wanting protection against the brute force of a dragon’s attacks, as well as its claws and fangs. On the other hand, wearing metal against something that breathed fire did seem as though it would turn the armor into a skillet with the knight trapped inside it.

Now stripped of his armor and unable to hurt me, the humanoid wolf experienced an existential crisis. He asked what he was supposed to do with himself. Knights were supposed to kill dragons, and wolves were supposed to eat sheep. I was both a sheep and a dragon, yet he could neither eat me nor kill me. If he could not fulfill either of the roles that seemed naturally assigned to him, he did not know what his purpose was.

I realized that purpose was what he was actually searching for, and I told him that this was an easy problem to solve. I offered to take him on as my apprentice.

“Apprentice?” he responded in disbelief. He asked what on earth I could possibly teach him. He did not have dragon wings, nor did he possess a sheep’s wool.

I told him that I could probably teach him how to fly.

Once again, he pointed out that he did not have wings like a dragon. I explained that the wings might not really be necessary. My own ability to fly did not seem to be based entirely upon my dragon wings. My unusual mist form came from my wool behaving like a cloud, which suggested that my powers arose partly from the association between sheep and clouds. I was drawing upon a more atmospheric, almost cosmic connection to my animal nature, and that symbolic connection allowed me to enter the sky.

Instead of asking the wolf to imitate my physical traits, I encouraged him to search for the cosmic association hidden within his own nature. I asked what wolves were connected with on a deeper level.

He considered the question and answered that wolves were associated with the moon because they howled at it.

I told him to use that connection to the moon and try to fly.

The wolf began channeling moonlight through his body. His formerly dark-brown fur transformed into pale, shimmering white fur that glowed with lunar energy. Drawing upon that newly discovered connection, he lifted himself from the ground and took flight without needing wings.

The two of us flew together across the medieval sky: the wolf as a radiant, pale-white, moonlit humanoid figure, and me as a humanoid black sheep-dragon with cloudlike wool and enormous batlike wings.

The dream left me with the realization that my purpose did not have to match what other people thought I was based upon my surface appearance. I could choose what I wanted to become by looking deeper, discovering a more profound aspect of who I already was, and embracing that truth instead of allowing society—or my most obvious features and attributes—to dictate what I had to be.
