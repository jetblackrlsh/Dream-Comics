The Vengeful Swarm

Before I went to sleep, I spotted a roach crawling across the ceiling. I grabbed some bug spray and killed it, but the incident stayed on my mind as I settled into bed.

I began thinking about ghosts and the idea that people can be haunted by those they wronged in life, especially if they did something terrible to them. Then another thought occurred to me: people kill bugs all the time. Most people probably have an enormous body count when it comes to insects.

If bug ghosts existed and wanted revenge, a person would not be haunted by only one spirit. They could be attacked by hundreds, thousands, or perhaps even more.

That thought experiment followed me into a lucid dream.

I found myself surrounded by countless ghost bugs seeking vengeance for their deaths. They rushed toward me from every direction in enormous waves. There were so many of them that the swarm seemed to flow like liquid, flooding the space around me as it closed in.

Rather than panic, I activated my lucid light.

Fighting every individual insect would have been nearly impossible, so I decided to reshape the swarm into a single target. My lucid light pulled all the ghost bugs together and compressed them into a humanoid body.

Despite being formed from a massive swarm, the creature was not gigantic. It stood only about six or seven feet tall. However, concentrating so many insects into one body gave it extraordinary abilities.

It possessed the combined strength, speed, agility, durability, instincts, and intelligence of countless species. Its body had the resilience of an exoskeleton. Its strength reflected insects capable of lifting many times their own body weight. Its mind operated with the coordinated intelligence of an advanced hive.

The ghost-bug humanoid charged at me with incredible speed.

Before I could react, it grabbed me by the neck and lifted me into the air.

Using its swarm intelligence, the creature somehow designed and constructed a computer chip almost instantaneously. The collective mind of the bugs understood engineering and technology far beyond what any individual insect—or even an ordinary person—should have been capable of understanding.

It implanted the chip into the back of my neck.

The device was an inhibitor. Its purpose was to prevent me from using my lucid light.

Without one of my primary dream powers, the situation appeared hopeless. I was facing an enemy with overwhelming strength, speed, agility, intelligence, and the ability to rapidly invent new technology, while my most versatile ability had been shut down.

Because I had gone to sleep very late, I repeatedly woke up during the night feeling that I had not gotten enough rest. Each time I returned to sleep, I found myself confronting the ghost-bug humanoid again.

The encounter became a repeating struggle.

Over and over, the creature reached me and implanted the inhibitor chip into the back of my neck. Each time, I tried to find a way around it. I experimented with different approaches, but I kept returning to the same problem: how could I escape an enemy that disabled my lucid light before I could properly fight back?

Eventually, after several failed attempts, I realized that I still had access to my tools.

Even without my lucid light, I could summon Psilones, my rainbow crystal sword and life sword. I also possessed Tetheron, my golden energy tether, although I decided it was not the right tool for this particular situation.

Summoning Psilones did not require me to use my inhibited power. It was more like reaching into a utility belt and pulling out equipment I already carried.

Psilones appeared on my back.

Because the sword possessed a life and will of its own, I did not need to physically draw or wield it. I simply called upon Psilones to act.

Instead of attacking the ghost-bug humanoid, Psilones turned toward the inhibitor chip embedded in the back of my neck and stabbed it.

As the life sword pierced the device, its power brought the chip itself to life.

The newly living chip pulled free from my neck, sprouted into motion, and crawled away under its own power.

The inhibition vanished.

My lucid light was available to me once again, but as I faced the ghost-bug humanoid, I realized I did not actually need it.

More importantly, I realized that fighting the creature had been the wrong approach from the beginning.

The bugs believed I had wronged them, and in a sense, they were right. I had killed them. They were not attacking me without reason. Their spirits were trapped by anger, pain, and a desire for vengeance.

They did not need to be punished.

They needed to be released.

I remembered that, as a teenager, I had become an ordained minister through the Universal Life Church. Over the years, I had also collected several honorary religious and mystical titles, including titles such as goddess and wizard. During college, I had even offered magical services through Fiverr, casting spells and blessing spaces for people who believed their homes or surroundings were cursed.

Drawing upon that part of my history, I chose not to attack the ghost-bug humanoid.

I blessed it.

The blessing passed through the collective form, reaching every insect spirit that composed it. Their anger dissolved. Their need for vengeance faded.

The humanoid shape evaporated into the air as the ghost bugs finally moved on into the afterlife.

By blessing them instead of destroying them, I ended the cycle of violence and allowed the spirits to find peace.

Then the dream ended.
