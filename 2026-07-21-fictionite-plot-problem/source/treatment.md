# Treatment

## Logline

When futuristic robotic bikers steal a museum crystal that supposedly makes impossible things possible, lucid dreamer Jet refuses the expected superhero battle and instead proves that no villain can explain what the crystal adds to a world where impossibility is already ordinary.

## Central conflict

The apparent conflict is a museum heist, but the real conflict is whether Fictionite has any meaningful purpose. The lead robotic biker expects Jet to behave like a conventional hero and fight for the stolen crystal. Jet instead asks a direct causal question: what new thing will the crystal let its owner do? Because the robot is already a fully thinking machine in a superpowered fictional world, neither he nor the later purple-haired thief can identify a useful answer. Jet tests the claim by inviting the woman to demonstrate one new feat, then demonstrates his own unrestricted dream power without touching Fictionite.

## Theme

A plot device matters only if it changes what characters can do, risk, or choose. Clear questions can dissolve a false conflict, and refusing an assigned heroic role can turn an enemy encounter into companionship.

## Emotional arc

Jet begins curious and contemplative, entering a museum rather than repeating his usual city flight. The violent heist creates the visual expectation of a superhero battle, but Jet remains calm. His refusal confuses the biker, and their confrontation becomes an honest discussion. The purple-haired woman's entrance briefly restores theatrical danger, yet her confidence collapses when Jet asks for evidence. Jet transforms the tension into absurd celebration, and the biker's posture changes from aggressive to thoughtful to relaxed. Jet ends amused, socially connected, and satisfied by good cake.

## Continuity

This dream belongs to the Storyverse continuity that began on June 11, 2025. Jet retains his established dark-brown skin, full beard, long dreadlocks, black hoodie, visible Dream Anime shirt, striped black pants, white sneakers, black belt bag, rainbow crystal sword, golden energy tether, and purple star-filled Lucid Light. His sword and tether remain secured and are not needed in this story.

Leon, Johnson, Second Brain, Skelebot, Overdrive, Savannah, Tecton, Chipper, and the Elven Village remain part of the wider continuity, but Jet arrives alone and none of them appear physically in this comic.

## Ending

The purple-haired woman drops Fictionite and runs from the birthday party Jet creates without it. Jet leaves the crystal untouched on the museum floor, shares chocolate ice cream cake with the robotic biker, and concludes that stealing it never made sense as a plot. The consequence is visible: the expected hero-villain battle has become a friendly celebration.

## Eight-page structure

1. Cover: Jet calmly studies glowing Fictionite while the robotic biker and purple-haired thief struggle theatrically around him; balloons and cake foreshadow his answer.
2. Jet induces lucidity, flies through an unfamiliar city, chooses a museum, imagines Fictionite, and finds it in a glass display.
3. Futuristic robotic bikers smash into the museum and steal the crystal, then become confused when Jet refuses to stop them.
4. Jet explains that a fully thinking, speaking robot is already an impossibility by waking-world standards and asks what the crystal would actually change.
5. The biker claims Fictionite should enable even more impossible feats, but Jet points to a world already full of robots, superpowers, and impossibilities; the biker begins reconsidering the theft.
6. A flamboyant purple-haired woman steals Fictionite and promises to show its true power, but freezes when Jet simply asks her to demonstrate one new ability.
7. Jet explains that he already has essentially unlimited power and summons balloons, two cakes, and a full birthday crowd; the woman panics, drops the crystal, and flees.
8. Jet and the robotic biker relax at the party, leave Fictionite unused, and share chocolate ice cream cake while Jet clearly states why the heist never worked as a plot.
