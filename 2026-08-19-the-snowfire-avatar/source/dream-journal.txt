Dream Journal Reconstruction
In my last couple of dreams before this one, I had been spending time in a magical study, thinking about how the Storyverse worked and writing down my thoughts and ideas. I wanted this dream to have another calm, contemplative beginning, but I did not want to return to exactly the same setting. I had been experimenting with different kinds of starting environments, so this time I found myself outside in a snowy wilderness.
Snow was falling all around me, but I was not cold. I was sitting comfortably on a purple couch with a fire pit burning in front of me. The fire was extraordinarily strong and seemed magical. Its heat protected the entire area around me, creating a warm sanctuary amid the freezing landscape. It felt almost as though I were sitting in a cozy living room that simply had no walls, surrounded on every side by snow and wilderness.
As I relaxed on the couch, I began thinking about role-playing games, which had recently become a major fixation for me. I had been learning about the history of role-playing games, the origins of Dungeons & Dragons, and the development of role-playing games as a genre. I had also been learning about MUSHes—multi-user shared hallucinations—and exploring how people role-play together online.
I thought about the interesting situation I was in because I could technically play role-playing games whenever I wanted by playing them with AI. At the same time, I sometimes wanted to play with other people, and arranging that seemed extremely difficult. The more I investigated how to play with other people, the more I encountered other people who were struggling with the same problem.
I had imagined that online role-playing would be easier than arranging games with friends, since scheduling conflicts could make playing with friends frustrating. I had pictured a large community where I could enter a forum, begin posting, and immediately find myself inside a game. Whenever someone wanted to continue from where I had left off, they could simply reply to my post.
Instead, much of what I found resembled a collection of wanted advertisements. People were looking for role-playing partners, but nearly everyone seemed to want something very specific. Even after joining a MUSH, much of my activity seemed to consist of role-playing as my character while sending mail messages to other characters to ask whether their players might be interested in doing a scene. It felt as though I were sending letters out into the world, but I had not yet received any replies. I had not checked that morning, but within the dream I was thinking about the experience of waiting.
I wondered whether I truly wanted to role-play with random strangers or whether part of what I enjoyed was simply writing letters as a role-playing character. Perhaps it could remain enjoyable even if no one ever replied and I just continued writing the letters. I knew that some solo role-playing games were built around responding to writing prompts and composing letters, so the letter-writing itself could potentially become its own game.
After sitting by the fire and thinking through all of this, I began to wonder whether the environment around me was too cozy. Although I had deliberately chosen not to return to the magical study, this snowy sanctuary served a similar purpose. Its appearance was completely different, but I questioned how different it truly was in practice.
I snapped my fingers, and the snowy wilderness instantly transformed into a hellscape. I remained seated on the same purple couch beside the same fire pit, but now a horde of flaming demons came running toward me.
I snapped my fingers again, restoring the snowy wilderness. The demons remained, but the extreme cold flash-froze all of them in place.
This gave me an interesting realization about the environment. The snowy wilderness must have been unimaginably cold if it was capable of freezing fire demons and even extinguishing or immobilizing the fires of Hell. At the same time, the magical flame in the fire pit must have been extraordinarily powerful if it could protect me from cold that severe while allowing me to sit comfortably beside it.
I suspected that the flame did not burn me because it was somehow connected or attuned to me. To test the idea, I reached into the fire pit and scooped up a handful of its flames. I could hold the fire directly in my hand without being burned.
Carrying the flame allowed me to leave the couch and walk safely through the snowy area. Its warmth continued protecting me from the extreme cold. I moved among the frozen fire demons, examining them where they stood. With all of them immobilized in the ice, it almost seemed as though I had decorated the wilderness with demonic statues.
As I inspected one of the frozen demons, I accidentally brought the flame in my hand too close to its face. The heat thawed the demon’s face while the rest of its body remained frozen. It suddenly lunged forward and bit my face off.
The moment sounded as though it should have been horribly gory, but underneath the missing flesh of my face was the face of a robot. I backed away from the demon, but I immediately became cognitively aware that I had a robotic face beneath my human appearance.
I considered how fitting that was. In my lucid dreams, it often seemed as though I was controlling an avatar. Lucidity gave me a strong awareness of the difference between the waking world and the dream world. I knew that my dream body and my physical body were distinct from one another. In that sense, my dream self could be understood as a robotic avatar that I piloted while my true physical body remained elsewhere. The demon had not torn off my physical face; it had merely damaged the outer face of my dream avatar and exposed the machinery underneath.
I wondered whether having a robotic layer beneath my face meant that I possessed other robotic abilities. I charged the diodes in my mechanical eyes and fired a laser blast at the frozen fire demon that had bitten my face.
When the laser struck the demon, the beam refracted through the ice surrounding it. The frozen fire demon then exploded.
I realized that the refraction could allow me to eliminate multiple demons with a single blast. I aimed another laser more deliberately, targeting a frozen demon positioned in the middle of a cluster. When the beam struck the ice, it split and refracted in multiple directions like light passing through a disco ball. Those beams struck the other frozen demons surrounding it, then continued cascading outward through the icy formation.
The entire snowy magical forest erupted into an incredible display of refracted laser light. One after another, the frozen fire demons exploded with enormous bursts of ice, color, flame, and light. The chain reaction became one of the most spectacular laser-light fireworks shows imaginable.
That beautiful, explosive celebration was the end of my dream.
