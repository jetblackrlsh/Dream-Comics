# Treatment

## Logline

While questioning whether a god can respect anyone else's free will, Jet follows a frightened flying sasquatch into a game-like superhero universe where a powerless archer proves that empathy and creative thought can accomplish what raw power cannot.

## Central conflict

Jet can reshape dream reality with Lucid Light, but he fears that using it to make hateful people loving would erase their ability to choose. The external superhero crisis turns that abstract dilemma into a practical test: godlike heroes fail when they rely only on force, while a mortal studies the problem and invents a compassionate solution that saves everyone.

## Theme

Power has no morality by itself. Empathy gives power a purpose, and creativity can achieve what domination cannot.

## Emotional arc

Jet begins thoughtful but unsettled, tempted by the apparent simplicity of changing people. He responds to the sasquatch's fear with empathy and adaptation rather than force, earning trust. Inside the electronic egg, he sees superpowers fail against two linked threats. Midnight Archer uses observation and imagination to turn the villain's own weapon into a rescue. Jet leaves with a clearer principle: do not control people into goodness; create possibilities that let them choose it.

## Ending

Back on the red couch in space, Jet holds the electronic egg while Midnight Archer's arrow-shaped light crosses its surface. He tells Doctor Bear that the best use of godlike power is not to command hearts, but to protect the space in which hearts can change.
